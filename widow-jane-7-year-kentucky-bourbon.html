The short version

An early sourced Widow Jane bourbon that helps explain the brand before its modern 10 Year blend. Reviews agree it is capable, while differing on intensity and value.

Exact identity: Historical seven-year Kentucky bourbon bottled in New York at 91 proof. Reviews from 2012 and 2016 concern this age/proof identity, not the later ten-year blend.

Background and history

The seven-year bottling documents Widow Jane’s early Kentucky-sourcing era. Christopher Null’s December 2012 review distinguishes the bourbon’s Kentucky distillation from its New York bottling.

That same review mentions a preceding five-year version. Evidence for that predecessor is too thin for an additional standalone dossier, so its existence is retained here as family history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

This historical identity carries seven years and 45.5% ABV. The Kentucky producer and full mash bill remain undisclosed. New York proofing water and bottling are documented; neither establishes a separate Brooklyn distillation origin.

Critical review synthesis

Christopher Null · Drinkhacker

Null praises the body and caramel-led richness but finds enough heat that water helps. His A-minus assessment is favorable.

Scope: December 2012 seven-year, 91-proof bottling.

Carin Moonin · The Whiskey Wash

Moonin describes a gentler earthy and saline presentation with pepper and citrus at the end, judging it well made rather than remarkable.

Scope: April 2016 seven-year bottling; source’s proof typo is disclosed.

Two independent historical samples differ. The summary records that difference rather than treating old barrels as a single invariant tasting result.
