The short version

An exceptionally well-supported excellent old corn whisky. Independent records emphasize balance, richness and careful wood management. Its substantial price remains a separate, much less favorable drinking-value question.

Exact identity: Canadian Club Chronicles Issue No. 5, The Icon, 45 years, 50% ABV / 100 proof / 750 mL; 2022 final Chronicles release.

Background and history

The Icon was announced October 26, 2022 as the fifth and final Chronicles expression, following Water of Windsor, Dock Man, Speakeasy and Whisky Sixes. The original 2017 forty-year is a separate predecessor.

The Canadian launch’s roughly CAD 500 price is kept distinct from U.S. evidence. A purchased Minnesota bottle was USD 309.99 / 750 mL in the April 2024 review; Utah’s official February 2026 quote is USD 369.99. Neither establishes an October 2026 local offer.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The October 2022 producer announcement authenticates 1977 barreling, 45 years and 50% ABV. Blair Phillips’s dedicated release report identifies the final expression as all corn, matured in ex-bourbon barrels that had subsequently held Canadian rye whisky. This editorial Single Grain grouping follows that specific recipe evidence; a generic blended-whisky brand footer does not supply an alternate recipe. The 41–44 additions are not transferred.

Critical review synthesis

Eric Burke · Bourbon Guy

Burke finds refined sweetness, floral and mature-oak character, careful balance and a medium-length finish. Neither strength nor wood overwhelms the pour; his purchased bottle is reserved for special occasions.

Scope: April 9, 2024, 45 years / 50% ABV, purchased 750 mL at Total Wine, Burnsville, Minnesota.

Jason Hambrey · Whisky Connosr

Hambrey’s blind judging sample stood out for purity, richness, deep aroma and restrained wood. He also notes that most fellow judges were less enthusiastic and that his own taste did not justify the premium price.

Scope: January 28, 2023 exact Chronicles No. 5 / 50% ABV; 97-point personal assessment, not a panel consensus.

André, Patrick, Martin and Kim · Québec Whisky

The named tasters consistently find inviting mature grain, fruit and wood, with substantial texture and good balance. Their notes differ in finish length and strength impact while remaining positive.

Scope: November 25, 2024 tasting entry, 50% ABV; later May 2025 site date is not a new production year.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Begin neat with a small pour and allow time in the glass to evaluate wood, grain and fruit integration.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops of water before a cube; protect the documented texture and subtle mature flavors.

Old Fashioned

A small Manhattan or restrained Old Fashioned is possible, but taste neat before deciding to sweeten an expensive release.

Highball

Use a small measure of soda if desired; the old-stock premium does not automatically make it a better mixing whisky.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
