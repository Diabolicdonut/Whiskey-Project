The short version

The original rye-and-rye-malt recipe is a separate historical whisky with powerful bread-like character, developing fruit and a long ending. Dryness and astringency remain part of the record.

Exact identity: Lot No. 40 original Canadian Whisky Guild-era recipe introduced in 1998, NAS, 43% ABV / 86 proof; original diagonal-label pack, reviewed as old stock in 2011.

Background and history

Introduced in 1998 under the Canadian Whisky Guild and discontinued before the modern 2012 revival. The 2011 old-stock review explicitly predates and links separately to the new edition.

Recipe and cask differences support a historical dossier. Classification is corrected to Canadian all-rye/single-grain: malted and unmalted rye do not imply different cereals.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The original Guild-era whisky was made at Hiram Walker with rye and rye malt and matured in reused American oak according to the detailed blender-visit history. The contemporary old-stock account also explicitly contrasts its lack of new-wood influence. This corrects discovery’s provisional new-oak description. Modern unmalted-only production and virgin-oak recipes are not transferred backwards. No numerical label age is authenticated.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The old-stock review describes an initially bread-led profile that opens into fruit and spice, with rich texture and a long peppery finish. Woody astringency, bitterness and unusual watery/pool-like aromas qualify its strong praise.
