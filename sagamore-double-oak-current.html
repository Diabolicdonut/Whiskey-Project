The short version

A sweet, heavily oak-influenced Maryland-style rye, with caramel, toasted coconut and nutty depth; independent reviewers disagree whether the second oak adds satisfying richness or obscures rye spice.

Exact identity: Standard ongoing Sagamore Double Oak Rye at 96.6 proof, finished 18 months in toasted wave-stave barrels; distinct from the published specific Batch 9A tasting dossier and individual Batch 8AF/8AP reviews.

Background and history

Double Oak is an ongoing core Sagamore release, not simply the exact Breaking Bourbon Batch 9A bottle already catalogued.

The producer's age description and current $59 distillery pickup offer distinguish the standing product from historical batch reviews and promotional award mentions.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer matures straight rye for four to five years in highly charred new American oak, then transfers it for 18 additional months to low-char, medium-plus toasted wave-stave oak with increased wood-contact area. The official 96.6-proof core label is distinct from a separately identified single batch.

Critical review synthesis

Brad · Secret Whiskey Society

In a specifically identified Batch 8AF 2024 bottle, describes caramel and vanilla with prominent charred oak, but criticizes one-dimensional texture and wood that hides rye's natural character.

Scope: Batch 8AF at 96.6 proof; not treated as sensory proof for every core bottle.

Open Pour editorial · Open Pour

In a general 96.6-proof review, identifies figs, cocoa and maple-sweet oak; finds clove and black pepper return on the finish, concluding that the second oak is well integrated.

Scope: General Double Oak expression; exact batch number not published.

Phil Dwyer · The Whiskey Wash

Notes rich pecan-like and sweet toasted-cask aroma with more recognizable spicy rye on the palate. Its retailer-style dollar figure is not used for the North Carolina Value benchmark.

Scope: September 2025 review of standard Double Oak, bottle/batch not independently matched to today's shelf stock.

Critical views legitimately conflict. Batch 8AF and Batch 9A are not automatically equal to an unnumbered bottle; no fresh firsthand tasting or inferred exact mash bill is claimed.

Serving recommendations

Neat

First assess neat: sweetness, rich wood and grain-spice interplay can be tested without dilution.

Rocks

A large cube softens wood and proof, but the heavier toast may remain dominant.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and aromatic bitters; this is a strong dessert-like Old Fashioned option only if more oak sweetness is desired.

Manhattan

Use a dryer vermouth and reduce cherry garnish sweetness; the finish can overwhelm lighter vermouths.

Highball

A highball is viable, but less efficient than a less expensive, unfancied rye if the finish is what warrants the premium.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
