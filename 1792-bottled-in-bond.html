How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: follow the development

1792 Bottled in Bond is 100 proof. Use a small tulip glass and compare the opening with the middle: vanilla and sweet oak with baking spice. The key question is whether that character persists as caramel meets firm pepper and wood.

Water: adjust in small steps

At 100 proof, taste neat before adding water; dilution is an experiment, not an improvement guaranteed by the label. The relevant finishing behavior is a sustained sweet-spicy finish. Stop when the mid-palate loses definition or dry wood begins to dominate.

Old Fashioned: match the sweetness

The documented middle is caramel meets firm pepper and wood. Begin with less sugar than usual and taste before adding more. At 100 proof, restrained dilution preserves the body; the cocktail should still taste of 1792 Bottled in Bond.

Rocks or a restrained Sour

A separate pour over one large cube lets you track the change from vanilla and sweet oak with baking spice toward a sustained sweet-spicy finish. For a Sour, balance citrus and sugar in a small trial; the documented finish is the part most easily obscured.
