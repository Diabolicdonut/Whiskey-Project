The short version

A gentle Cognac-cask-finished blend with sharply differing judgments about depth and persistence. The finish is verified, while its duration and complete grain recipe remain undisclosed.

Exact identity: Crown Royal XO, recurring NAS Canadian blend finished in Cognac casks, 40% ABV / 80 proof; introduced January 2014.

Background and history

The producer announcement describes an early-January 2014 national introduction at USD 49.99 / 750 mL. XO is the marketed expression name, not a Canadian label age or a claim to Cognac age rules.

The current NC listing independently identifies the recurring XO at 80 proof and USD 49.95 / 750 mL, code 37-905. The historical and current price contexts are distinguished.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The January 16, 2014 producer release credits master blender Andrew MacKay and more than fifty whiskies, followed by finishing in Cognac casks made from Limousin oak. Current producer documentation confirms 40% ABV. The Cognac-cask treatment is not a disclosed addition of liquid Cognac. No label age, finish duration or Cognac-house identity is authenticated.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

De Kergommeaux finds substantial creamy body, mature wood and layered fruit/spice development, with a persistent ending. His sample supplies the strongest depth evidence.

Scope: June 19, 2014; exact XO at 40%, separate from Cask No. 16.

Bobby · Adventures in Whiskey

Bobby finds a step up in flavor from the flagship but too light in weight and short in the finish for his preference, contrasting with the richer specialist sample.

Scope: February 3, 2016; exact XO section at 40%, disclosed Diageo sample; XR section kept separate.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Neat first makes the reported difference in body and finish easier to judge.

Rocks / water

Use a few drops or one cube; it is already forty percent.

Old Fashioned / Manhattan

A restrained Old Fashioned is an editorial option; heavy mixing obscures the finish.

Highball

A short soda highball can work, though the flagship is a less expensive mixing reference.
