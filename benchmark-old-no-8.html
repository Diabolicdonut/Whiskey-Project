Benchmark Old No. 8 Bourbon

A useful below-average quality reference that remains inexpensive enough to have a clear budget role.

The short version

Below average neat bourbon; excellent low-cost utility value.

Benchmark Old No. 8 is a clear example of why a whiskey can score below average for category Quality and still offer strong Value. Recent reviews find muted aroma, thin texture and limited development, with grain or bitter dry oak intruding in the more critical account. At $10.95 NC, those limits come with a genuinely low purchase cost. 345

The subject is the standard black-label 80-proof Old No. 8, not Benchmark Bonded, Full Proof, Top Floor or another extension. Their strengths and age claims are separate. Its Buffalo Trace association is not an automatic quality bonus.

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

Benchmark’s older brand history and the McAfee name provide context, but its current role is an entry-priced Buffalo Trace bourbon. The producer presents Old No. 8 separately from the expanded Benchmark range. That distinction prevents a buyer from using a higher-proof extension’s review as evidence for this standard bottle. 1

Thomas Fondano’s earlier budget review is positive, particularly at the low cost. Later detailed reviews are less persuasive about neat quality. This is a meaningful disagreement about standards and use, rather than proof that an inexpensive bottle must be either a hidden masterpiece or a drain pour. 6

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The standard reviewed bottle is 80 proof with a three-year label. This age is documented in the independent label descriptions; a conflicting catalog age field is not adopted automatically. The precise grain formula is undisclosed, with low-rye Mash Bill #1 commonly reported. 36

At 40% ABV, the bottle has less dilution reserve than the 100/101-proof budget staples. That is useful serving context, not a score penalty by itself. The sensory limitation is its thinness and weak development, which must be distinguished from the number on the label.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Muted vanilla, apple or cinnamon, with grain and light oak; the aroma can be less rewarding than the producer name suggests.

Palate

Simple sweetness and grain, thin texture, and dry/bitter oak in the more critical account.

Finish

A faded or limited finish, with occasional sustained heat or dry grain rather than evolving depth.

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon · March 24, 2022

Jordan Moskal finds the standard bottle slightly below average: acceptable basic flavors but muted aroma, thinness and an unremarkable finish. He does not describe the severe off-note imbalance of the bottom bands. 3

Alex Wang · The Whiskey Shelf

Alex Wang is more critical of grain and dry bitterness. His account strengthens the below-midpoint placement, especially as a neat whiskey, without treating the low price as a sensory benefit. 4

Thomas Fondano · Bourbon & Banter · September 17, 2014

Thomas Fondano enjoys vanilla/honey and modest fruit, recommending the bottle for value. He still calls it his least-favored low-rye Buffalo Trace bourbon, an important limit within the positive assessment. 6

Where the judgments differ

The positive budget review evaluates an agreeable inexpensive pour, while the more critical accounts focus on thinness and dry grain. The synthesis is below-average neat quality at 4.2, with no need to allege catastrophic defects. Price belongs in the separate 8.0 Value score.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Neat preference and restrained sugar/vermouth guidance come directly from Thomas Fondano. Ice, highball and pairing suggestions are editorial inferences.

Neat / water

Fondano prefers it neat and advises against adding much water to an 80-proof bourbon. A small tasting establishes whether the dry grain suits you.

On ice

Use a large cube sparingly; thinness becomes more evident with extended dilution.

Old Fashioned

Fondano recommends restrained sugar. A modest bitters measure helps preserve what bourbon flavor is present.

Manhattan

The same review suggests less vermouth than usual. This is a deliberate light-bourbon adjustment rather than proof of premium mixing depth.

Highball / cola

A plausible low-cost simple mixer, but avoid excessive dilution if you want recognizable whiskey character.

Food / occasion

Useful for a budget comparison flight or uncomplicated mixed service, less persuasive as a focused neat showcase.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

Bourbon Quality
3.9 / 10

Breaking Bourbon finds muted flavor and a short, one-dimensional close; the Whiskey Shelf adds harsh grain and bitter dryness, while Bourbon & Banter supplies a genuine budget-positive counterpoint. That leaves Benchmark below the Maker’s/Bulleit midpoint, but well above Cleveland’s persistent chemical defects. Its recognizable sweetness prevents a poor-end score; Cabin Still is similarly limited, with less reported harshness.

Value · NC ABC
7.0 / 10

At $10.95, it remains a useful inexpensive mixer. The thin, occasionally bitter neat profile is a meaningful compromise beside Evan Williams Bottled-in-Bond at $21.95; very good budget value fits better than an unqualified excellent claim.

Quality is category-relative and excludes price or personal-palate weighting. Value uses the identified $10.95 750 mL reference. The scores are editorial synthesis, not averages of incompatible published rating systems.

Quality scale: 1 catastrophic · 2 extremely poor · 3 poor · 4 below average · 5 average · 6 good · 7 very good · 8 excellent · 9 elite · 10 reference-class. Quality scores are category-relative. A 9.0 Bourbon means elite within Bourbon; a 9.0 Rye means elite within Rye. The numbers are not claims that unlike whiskey styles taste equivalent.
