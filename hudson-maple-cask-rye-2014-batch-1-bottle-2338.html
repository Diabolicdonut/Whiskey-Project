Hudson Maple Cask Rye — 2014 Batch 1, Bottle 2338

The short version

A provisional failure sample, with same-batch disagreement.

Geoff Kleinman’s November 2014 update identifies Batch 1, Bottle 2338 and describes an increasingly punishing progression of raw oak, heat and an actively unpleasant finish. That level of documented failure makes it a useful practical low-reference candidate for American rye. Only one independent critic identifies this exact bottle, so its provisional 1.0 cannot be treated as an established verdict on the full batch. 1

Thomas Øhrbom reviews 2014 Batch 1 as well, but finds balanced sweetness, a silky palate and a long minty finish, awarding 84/100. His bottle number is not supplied. That is a substantial contradiction, not a footnote to erase; all rye quality scores in this collection remain provisional while the low endpoint is unsettled. 3

The expanded review check finds additional harsh-oak criticism and favorable bottles from both early and later periods. Confidence in a recurring historical flaw has improved; confidence that the expression is the category’s worst has not. Review evidence and limits.

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

Maple Cask Rye belongs to the earlier Tuthilltown/Hudson period, when small-barrel whiskey and a compact bottle helped distinguish the New York producer. It is not the current green-label Do The Rye Thing, and it is not the later Short Stack expression. Distiller marks Maple Cask discontinued and records a replacement in September 2020. 4

The ownership timeline also needs two dates. Ralph Erenzo’s account distinguishes selling the Hudson brand in 2010 from retaining the distillery until the 2017 transaction. For a 2014 bottle, the original production era matters more than current corporate ownership or new packaging. 5

The finish was a round trip for used whiskey barrels: they went to a maple-syrup producer, held syrup, then returned for whiskey finishing. It was a cask treatment rather than evidence of a measured dose of maple flavoring. A distinctive process can explain a product’s identity without guaranteeing a balanced result. 34

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The reviewed historical expression is 92 proof, or 46% ABV. Its secondary casks previously held maple syrup. The exact age, initial barrel size, grain percentages and finishing duration of Bottle 2338 are not established by the sample-specific account, so they remain undisclosed rather than borrowed from later Hudson whiskey. 1

Kleinman connects his oak complaint with small-barrel aging, but the actual cause of the harsh impressions is not independently demonstrated. His language about distillation errors is a reviewer’s diagnosis, not a laboratory finding. This dossier records the sensory criticism without turning it into a confirmed defect mechanism. 1

The category is American rye with a named finish. Maple finishing adds sweetness and wood effects to a rye base; it does not turn this into a directly interchangeable maple liqueur. Neither the current producer’s 95/5 recipe nor the three-year minimum for Do The Rye Thing establishes the contents of this historical sample.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

In the severe sample account, sharp raw wood dominates the nose and obscures much of the maple. Øhrbom instead finds vanilla, mint and approachable sweetness in his same-batch bottle.

Palate

The Bottle 2338 report begins with light maple and caramel before intrusive oak and heat overwhelm it. The favorable same-batch account emphasizes silky sweetness controlled by peppery rye.

Finish

The exact-sample account describes an actively unpleasant, hot close. Other historical criticism also finds punitive tannin, while the positive 2014 review describes a rich, long peppermint close. There is no honest single consensus finish.

Geoff Kleinman · Drink Spirits · December 2013; update November 2014

The 2014 update is the only full source that identifies Bottle 2338. It says the new sample fails to improve the earlier release and finds a sharply unpleasant finish. That sample specificity is why it supplies the provisional floor. 1

Thomas Øhrbom · Whisky Saga · December 28, 2014

Øhrbom identifies 2014 Batch 1 and finds the sweetness and spice integrated, with a silky mouthfeel and a long finish. His 84/100 is direct same-batch counterevidence, even though the bottle number is missing. 3

Margarett Waterbury · The Whiskey Wash · October 2, 2015

Waterbury’s 2015 review finds an attractive sweet entry that collapses under charred, tannic oak. It supports a historical failure mode, but cannot independently corroborate Bottle 2338 because the matching batch is not documented. 6

Amanda Schuster · Distiller · undated expert assessment

Schuster’s undated expert assessment sees restrained maple and rye sweetness, then overly dominant wood. This mixed account is another reason to avoid assigning the sample’s extreme score to the whole expression. 4

Where the judgments differ

The evidence cannot establish whether bottle variation, batch inconsistency, palate sensitivity or serving conditions explain the opposite same-batch judgments. The provisional score records the severity reported for one identifiable sample. Establishing a category floor requires independent corroboration or a better documented catastrophic rye reference; later reviews with unmatched batches do not settle the issue.

Consumer context

Distiller’s community rating describes many unidentified bottles, not Bottle 2338. It can show that the broader expression did not receive uniform rejection, but cannot repair the missing exact-sample evidence. Collecting an old bottle for historical interest is also a different decision from buying it for pleasure in the glass.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: The positive reviewer supplies a dessert suggestion. Other serving comments are explicitly conservative editorial inferences; no credible exact-Bottle 2338 cocktail or dilution test was located. 3

Neat research sample

Use a small neat sample to identify which reported profile an actual bottle resembles. There is no reliable basis to promise that all 2014 Batch 1 bottles taste like the low-reference account.

Incremental water

A few drops may test the balance, but no controlled water comparison establishes a rescue for Bottle 2338. The favorable review does not prove dilution caused the disagreement.

One cube

Cold and dilution may mute intensity, but masking raw oak is different from creating integration. This is a cautious editorial inference, not a documented improvement.

Old Fashioned

For a maple-and-rye drink, a competent rye with measured maple syrup offers controllable sweetness. Additional sugar is unlikely to restore a whiskey dominated by oak; this is an inference from the negative reviews.

Dessert / food

Øhrbom recommends his favorable bottle as a dessert-like pour. That recommendation belongs to his sample and should not be transferred to the negatively reviewed Bottle 2338. 3

Less ideal uses

Avoid purchasing a full old bottle solely because it is the provisional floor. Historical curiosity does not establish drinking value, and no cocktail recommendation overcomes the unresolved bottle identity.

Quality is category-relative and excludes price or personal-palate weighting. Value uses the identified $88.00 750 mL reference. The scores are editorial synthesis, not averages of incompatible published rating systems.

Quality scale: 1 catastrophic · 2 extremely poor · 3 poor · 4 below average · 5 average · 6 good · 7 very good · 8 excellent · 9 elite · 10 reference-class. Quality scores are category-relative. A 9.0 Bourbon means elite within Bourbon; a 9.0 Rye means elite within Rye. The numbers are not claims that unlike whiskey styles taste equivalent.
