Background and history

The commercial Rare Cask release was available by May 2017; TWS’s May 15 preview and a May 30 purchased-bottle account support the chronology. It is distinct from the underlying scientific dissertation.

Approximately twelve years is an attributed stock estimate, not the NAS label’s age statement. The 46.1% strength alludes to ethanol’s molar mass; it is not evidence of analytical purity or a production strength.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The exact Rare Cask Series label and matched accounts verify 46.1% and NAS. The blend draws on Livermore’s aging-research stock, with 87% rye widely reported. TWS’s pre-release specification describes virgin oak with two char levels; other accounts describe the broader experiment’s varied casks. Those research casks do not automatically establish every final-blend component. Reports of sixty versus seventy-eight selected barrels conflict, so no precise final barrel count is asserted.

Critical review synthesis

Selfbuilt · Selfbuilt’s Whisky Analysis

Selfbuilt praises developed fruit, assertive spice and silky texture with remarkably little bitter oak. The medium-long finish still falls short of the persistence he wants.

Scope: July 2, 2018 exact NAS / 46.1% Dissertation; his aggregate review table is not additional inspected evidence.

Whisky Lassie · Whisky Lassie

The purchased-bottle account finds a vivid rye/floral nose and creamy, complex palate, followed by a pleasant spicy ending that is shorter than expected.

Scope: August 11, 2017 review of bottles purchased May 30; final barrel-count claim conflicts with the pre-release account.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Rest a small neat pour to assess fruit, spice, texture and the drying finish in the published accounts.

Water / rocks

Add drops gradually or one small cube, comparing aroma and texture as it dilutes.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and bitters. A recurring Canadian blend is the cost comparison before mixing a limited bottle.

Manhattan / highball

Try a small serve with restrained vermouth or soda; a softer whisky can lose definition under assertive mixers.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
