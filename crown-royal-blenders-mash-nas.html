Background and history

Contemporary sources place the original Bourbon Mash naming in March 2018 and document the subsequent Blenders’ Mash relabeling. The naming history is recorded from those sources without treating the Canadian product as bourbon.

The September 2018 in-depth review is explicitly NAS / 80 proof, while the May 2018 review uses the renamed Blenders’ Mash label at 40%. The original U.S. USD 30 MSRP remains historical context; no current exact NC listing is assumed.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The 2018 exact sources document a blend of corn-heavy whiskies matured in new and used American oak, bottled at 40% ABV. This Canadian blend is not bourbon. The photographed Blenders’ Series NAS bottle reads 40% / 80 proof. The producer’s current page pairs 40% text with a 45% / 90-proof image; that mismatched image is excluded in favor of the independently verified eighty-proof label.

Critical review synthesis

Bobby · Adventures in Whiskey

Bobby enjoys a subdued but pleasant aroma, rounded sweetness and light fruit/spice, with a clean ending. He recommends it as an approachable pour rather than a challenging one.

Scope: May 16, 2018; renamed NAS Blenders’ Mash 40% ABV, producer-provided sample; exact 80-proof photograph authenticated.

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon

Moskal finds the aroma promising but a dry, muddled and weak palate/finish. He regards the USD 30 drinking proposition as poor despite its unusual labeling history.

Scope: September 6, 2018; NAS, 80 proof, original Bourbon Mash alias, bottle 0195470; substantive sample counterevidence.
