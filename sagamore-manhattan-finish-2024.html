The short version

A 103-proof rye reinterpreting a Manhattan cocktail through three finishing cask types: herbal, cherry-tinged and baking-spicy, though critics disagree on how generously cocktail-like sweetness survives its drying rye finish.

Exact identity: The second Sagamore Manhattan Finish release, announced March 26, 2024; 103 proof and at least 20 months in separately used vermouth, bitters and cherry-brandy barrels. Distinct from 2020 and the 2025 30-month version.

Background and history

The inaugural Manhattan Finish arrived as a 2020 Distiller's Select; the producer announced a second separately dated release on March 26, 2024.

A 2025 reserve edition used 30 months of finishing and deserves its own identity. Packaging and a generic Manhattan Finish review should not erase that distinction.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Sagamore used four-year-old straight rye and divided finishing among separate vermouth, bitters, and cherry-brandy barrels for at least 20 additional months before reblending and bottling at 103 proof.

Critical review synthesis

Stephanie Moreno · Distiller

Finds candied vanilla, cherry and orange on the nose but a more peppery, tight and somewhat tannic palate; vermouth/cherry influence is restrained against assertive grain spice.

Finds a more favorable balance of cherry, herbaceous vermouth and spice and reviews this specifically identified 2024 release. Publisher discloses a producer-provided sample.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Best first enjoyed neat to understand the actual cask-supplied bitters and cherry-brandy flavors rather than masking them with cocktail ingredients.

Rocks

One large cube tempers rye pepper and dryness but may diminish nuanced cherry/brandy finish.

Old Fashioned

Go easy on sweetener: this rye already brings cherry-brandy and bitters cask impressions.

Manhattan

Try neat first, then a reduced-vermouth Manhattan; a full conventional cocktail can duplicate the finish components and become muddled.

Highball

A highball is viable, but less efficient than a less expensive, unfancied rye if the finish is what warrants the premium.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
