The short version

A restrained, well-integrated mature bourbon with a real Mizunara contrast. Its roughly USD 500 price is far harder to defend than its execution.

Exact identity: First Black Opal release, 2024; 20-year blend finished in Mizunara, 99 proof. The 2026 thirteen-month finish is a separate identity.

Background and history

Black Opal was introduced in 2024 above the annual Vaults range. Its twenty-year base and Japanese-oak secondary treatment mark a distinct older-age expression.

The initial allocation was 5,000 bottles. The steel presentation box and rarity describe the release, but do not supply Quality or drinking-Value points.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer documents a blend of bourbons aged at least twenty years, finished in medium-toasted, lightly charred old-growth Mizunara casks, then bottled at 49.5% ABV. Breaking Bourbon identifies Kentucky and Tennessee sourcing. The exact mash bills, blend proportions and initial finish duration are undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon

Moskal finds berry-pastry aromas and nutty custard sweetness with gentle spice. The long-aged base avoids excessive wood, but the light overall delivery is restrained for the price.

Scope: 2024 first release; December 2024 review.

Stephanie Moreno · Distiller

Moreno recommends time in the glass, describing toasty spice, caramel-apple sweetness and herbal tobacco. She reports a medium body and a relatively long ending.

Scope: 2024 edition, 49.5% ABV.

Quality and calibration

Quality 7.0 / 10. Both named reviews support mature sweetness, spice and controlled oak. Modest intensity and a medium body keep the dossier at very good rather than exceptional; twenty years and rare cooperage are not substitutes for concentration.
