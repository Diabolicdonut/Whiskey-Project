Background and history

The programme emerged in the 2010 era. De Kergommeaux explicitly notes earlier Canadian single-barrel precedents, so a marketing claim to be the world’s first is not repeated as settled historical fact.

Its recurring single-barrel identity is distinct from individual retailer picks. The August–October 2026 NC standard entry is code 19-620 at USD 56.95 / 750 mL; the BTB programme entry is not treated as a new expression.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon

Moskal finds an easy, airy pour with sweet vanilla/caramel flavors and a short finish. He would like more concentration and depth.

Scope: October 23, 2018; 80 proof, NAS; production-bottle sample provided by Sazerac.

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

De Kergommeaux finds a richer creamy sample with wood, fruit, citrus and developing spice. He praises it strongly while noting that its texture partly obscures definition and that single barrels can vary.

Scope: Historical 40% ABV programme sample; not assumed to be the same barrel as the 2018 review.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat to preserve the low-proof detail and assess your barrel.

Rocks / water

Use limited water or one cube; the 2018 sample was already light.

Old Fashioned / Manhattan

Keep sugar and vermouth restrained so a gentle barrel remains audible.

Highball

A short soda highball suits the gentle style; compare a cheaper blend for heavy mixing.
