The short version

A fruity, experimental used-oak whiskey whose reviews disagree about refinement. Its traditional applewood identity should not be confused with the later straight-rye blend.

Exact identity: Legacy 91-proof whiskey distilled from a rye mash, aged with applewood staves in used bourbon oak; the detailed 2020 review is batch 40. Modern 93-proof Apple Wood straight rye is separate.

Background and history

The legacy range uses the label whiskey distilled from a rye mash, while the later 93-proof product uses a blend of straight ryes. The changed base/process is the reason for separate records.

Whisky Monster’s batch-40 account is less persuaded by neat-drinking balance than Kara Newman’s later legacy-release assessment. Both remain attributed and time-bound.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The 91-proof identity uses a rye-based mash with undisclosed percentages. Published descriptions establish at least two years in used bourbon barrels with applewood staves. New-charred-oak straight-rye compliance is not inferred from its flavor or a retailer’s shorthand.

Critical review synthesis

Whisky Monster · Whisky Monster

The batch-40 account finds fresh apple/citrus and spice, but also prominent young spirit character and incomplete balance. It sees a stronger highball/cocktail role than a neat-drinking case.

Scope: December 2020, batch 40, 45.5% ABV.

Kara Newman · Wine Enthusiast

Newman reports maple/vanilla aromas, a drying brown-sugar and tea palate, soft leather and a pepper/citrus ending. Her later 93-point legacy-release result is more favorable.

Scope: March 2022, 45.5% ABV; legacy label, not the 93-proof modern blend.
