Background and history

Campari’s September 17, 2021 launch announcement calls Master’s Cut its first cask-strength whisky. The term describes the producer’s bottling treatment; a lower cask-strength ABV is not by itself a defect or proof of dilution.

Original Canadian announced retail was CAD 79.95. Exact 2021 identity is preserved rather than substituting a different Forty Creek bottling or a size-unspecified U.S. import reference.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The contemporary producer statement specifies a single pass through pot stills and maturation in once-used bourbon and experienced Canadian-whisky barrels. The archive verifies five years and 48.5%. Megawatt’s attributed research describes redistilled mature stocks; it is not evidence that an earlier maturation period can be added to the five-year statement. Ex-bourbon wood does not turn the final blend into bourbon.

Critical review synthesis

Megawatt · Whisky Connosr

Megawatt finds a restrained but developing aroma, substantial body and an unusually persistent, evolving finish. Fruit, nuts and controlled bitter wood contribute to an enthusiastic assessment.

Scope: October 10, 2021; explicit Lot 5 / 48.5%.

André · Québec Whisky

André likes the expressive grain and spice balance after airing, but finds the finish shorter than expected. His judgment is positive without simply rewarding strength.

Scope: December 31, 2021 page; exact 2021 edition / 48.5%.

Patrick and Martin · Québec Whisky

Patrick appreciates the oiliness but finds the balance wanting. Martin hears less aromatic and mid-palate definition than the good ending promises. Their more reserved accounts constrain the enthusiastic sample.

Scope: Two additional complete accounts on the same publisher page; not counted as separate publishers.
