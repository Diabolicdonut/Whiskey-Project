The short version

An inexpensive, lightly built Canadian blend with contrasting reviews: one favors its easy fruit and rye-spice balance, another finds it sharp and best mixed. Its name does not specify proof.

Exact identity: Seagram’s 83 Canadian Whisky, 40% ABV / 80 proof, NAS. This is the Canadian brand associated with the 1883 lineage, not Seagram’s 83 American blended whiskey at 41.5% ABV.

Background and history

LCBO connects the name to Joseph Seagram’s Original Rye of 1883. That is brand lineage, not a distillation date or the age of a modern bottle.

The Seagram name predates the current owner. The 2018 sale announcement and Sazerac’s subsequent account establish the ownership transition; neither proves that every modern component was distilled at a particular plant. Historical Valleyfield accounts are kept in their own era.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Current control-board listings establish Canadian origin, 40% ABV and 750 mL. Historical specialist reporting locates production at Diageo’s Valleyfield operation in 2013. Sazerac now owns the brand, but an exact current plant, mash recipe, cask schedule and component ages are not authenticated.

Critical review synthesis

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Finds lively spice and sweet grain-fruit notes, followed by a sharp, drying delivery. The flavors come together more successfully with ice and ginger ale than when tasted neat.

Scope: April 21, 2013, Diageo-era sample; its bottle-presentation points are excluded from Quality.

Jason Hambrey · Whisky Connosr

Finds a simple but pleasant light blend with fruit, sweetness and a spicy grain backbone. Rates the balance and finish more favorably than Dykstra; a little bitterness remains.

Scope: January 7, 2015 posting of earlier notes; claimed Crown Royal barrels are not accepted as authenticated production evidence.

Serving recommendations

Neat

A small neat trial is enough to check tolerance for the documented sharpness.

Rocks

A little ice may soften its spice; stop before its light body disappears.

Old Fashioned

Keep sugar low and use a short build.

Manhattan

Try a small 2:1 build; the modest body may be overwhelmed by richer vermouth.

Highball

Ginger ale is the most directly supported use in Dykstra’s account.
