The early standard release divides critics sharply. Its favorable evidence does not support forcing it into the below-average seed band.

The short version

Competent to good, with real disagreement about youthful grain.

Blaum Bros’ early self-distilled standard bourbon is more complicated than its seed placement near band 4 suggests. Breaking Bourbon finds intrusive young grain and little development. Whiskeyfellow finds an oily, integrated fruit-and-spice bourbon, while Bourbon & Banter lands between those views. A 5.8 score reflects that evidence without pretending consensus is stronger than it is. 123

The subject is the 100-proof standard expression reviewed in 2019–20. It is separate from the sourced Knotter line and from later single barrels or revised batches. Historical MSRP is used because a current bottle with the same name does not automatically represent this early review era.

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

Mike and Matt Blaum built the Galena business while selling sourced whiskey under the Knotter name and allowing their own distillate to mature. The early straight bourbon marked that transition from barrel selection to a house-made spirit. Reputation earned by the sourced line should not be transferred automatically to the new bourbon. 2

The competing 2019–20 assessments provide a useful lesson in craft calibration: a noticeable cereal note can read as lively grain to one drinker and insufficient maturation to another. The dossier preserves both reactions rather than treating the lowest or highest review as definitive.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The reported recipe is 72% corn, 23% rye and 5% malted barley, matured for at least four years and bottled at 100 proof. Whiskeyfellow reports a hybrid pot still, 117.5 entry proof and eight-barrel batches for the early release. These are dated review-era details, not guaranteed specifications for every subsequent batch. 2

The higher rye portion helps explain the spice-led profile, but a mash bill cannot predict an exact taste. Nor does four years by itself guarantee integrated oak. The key distinction is whether sweetness, grain, spice and tannin work together in the glass.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Caramel, fruit and baking spice in favorable reviews; younger cereal and muted fruit in the harsher assessment.

Palate

The positive account finds coating texture and balanced fruit/spice. The negative review finds grain taking over with little rewarding development.

Finish

Opinions range from lingering spice and bread to a short, simple ending. Finish is a major point of disagreement.

Eric Hasman · Breaking Bourbon · November 22, 2020

Eric Hasman’s 1.5/5 review finds the grain too youthful and the profile underdeveloped. This is a meaningful critical warning about the early standard release, not grounds to condemn every Blaum product. 1

Jeffrey Schwartz · Whiskeyfellow · May 27, 2019

Jeffrey Schwartz recommends a bottle after a supplied-sample tasting. He finds an oily mouthfeel, caramel and fruit, then a sustained spicy finish. This independent positive account prevents a confident band-4 consensus. 2

Jim Knudsen · Bourbon & Banter · September 24, 2019

Jim Knudsen finds an enjoyable, relatively approachable bourbon with grain and cinnamon. Limited complexity and a shorter finish moderate the praise, positioning the release between the two more polarized assessments. 3

Where the judgments differ

Distinct batches, tasting context and tolerance for grain-forward flavor may explain some disagreement, but exact barrel matching is unavailable. The appropriate conclusion is modestly above average with medium confidence. A separate, better-supported bottle fills the below-average calibration slot.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Serving guidance is editorial inference from the documented 100-proof, grain-and-spice structure. The three written reviews are sensory evidence rather than bartender consensus.

Neat / water

Try neat first, then a few drops if spice obscures fruit. Batch uncertainty makes a small trial more useful than assuming improvement.

On ice

100 proof gives some dilution reserve. One large cube is a plausible relaxed serve, especially with a grain-forward batch.

Old Fashioned

Cinnamon and rye spice should survive restrained sugar and bitters; this is an inference from the reviews.

Manhattan

A modest vermouth measure may support fruit without exaggerating bread/cereal notes. No named bartender test was established.

Highball / cola

A chilled highball can work with a spicy standard bourbon, but long dilution may expose a short finish.

Food / occasion

A craft-versus-established-distillery flight is more informative than buying solely for the brand’s sourced-whiskey reputation.
