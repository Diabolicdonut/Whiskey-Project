The short version

A vibrant, complex old Canadian blend, with fruit and spice from documented additions. Strong reviews support very good execution, while an important counterpoint finds the wood near its limit and prefers the earlier pure-corn release.

Background and history

The Water of Windsor inaugurated the Chronicles series in 2018, following the separate 2017 forty-year release. Its name points to Windsor, Ontario, the whisky’s production lineage.

A February 2020 U.S.-market account records USD 250 / 750 mL SRP. That dated historical context is distinct from the Canadian suggested price and from current collector listings.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer confirms the 1977 corn-stock lineage and Cognac, rye and sherry additions. WhiskyCast identifies August–September 1977 Hiram Walker distillation and American-oak maturation. The original forty-year parcel continued aging before the 2018 blending; this is a different recipe from the 2017 forty-year. Exact addition proportions are not inferred.

Critical review synthesis

Mark Gillespie · WhiskyCast

Gillespie finds integrated grain spice and fruit, followed by a lively palate and a long dry ending. Astringency remains part of the finish, but the overall judgment is strongly favorable.

Scope: November 2018, 41-year / 45% ABV, 94-point published judgment.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra finds well-combined nutty sweetness, fruit and spice but says persistent sappy wood is approaching the whisky’s limit. He likes the blend while preferring less influence from the added spirits and wine.

Scope: January 12, 2020, exact 41-year / 45% ABV; 89.5 points include presentation as well as sensory execution.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Begin neat with a small pour and allow time in the glass to evaluate wood, grain and fruit integration.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops of water before a cube; protect the documented texture and subtle mature flavors.

Old Fashioned

A small Manhattan or restrained Old Fashioned is possible, but taste neat before deciding to sweeten an expensive release.

Highball

Use a small measure of soda if desired; the old-stock premium does not automatically make it a better mixing whisky.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
