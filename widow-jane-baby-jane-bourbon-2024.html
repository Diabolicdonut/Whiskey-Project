The short version

An approachable heirloom-corn blend with real Brooklyn distillate, but its light intensity and disputed integration keep the Quality assessment near average-to-good.

Exact identity: National Baby Jane blend launched June 2024, 91 proof. Breaking Bourbon reviewed batch 48; the one-year 2016 anniversary Baby Jane is separately documented.

Background and history

The nationally distributed 2024 Baby Jane uses the proprietary corn descended from Bloody Butcher and Wapsie Valley. This follows earlier experimental heirloom and very young Baby Jane releases, rather than marking the first time Widow Jane ever distilled bourbon.

The modern bottling combines Brooklyn and Kentucky whiskey. The name’s agricultural story does not authenticate an undisclosed blend proportion or a numerical mash bill for the whole blend.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The authenticated launch identity is a blend of straight bourbons distilled by Widow Jane in Brooklyn and sourced from Kentucky. The producer reports four-to-six-year components, while the bottle is NAS. It is non-chill filtered and bottled at 45.5% ABV. Full blend proportions and mash percentages are undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Nick Beiter · Breaking Bourbon

Beiter finds light fruit and honey at the start, balanced apricot/caramel sweetness in the middle and a more satisfying spiced-pastry ending. He regards it as accessible rather than a forceful showcase of heirloom grain.

Scope: Batch 48; review July 2024.

Stephanie Moreno · Distiller

Moreno describes toasted grain and herbs beside vanilla, with a slick palate and pepper at the end. She is less persuaded by how the grassy and sweet parts integrate.

Scope: 2024 national 91-proof release; batch not specified.

Quality and calibration

Quality 5.7 / 10. Published evidence supports clean, agreeable fruit and sweetness but limited intensity; Moreno’s concern about integration tempers the positive finish reported by Beiter. The result is above competent, short of firmly very good.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Begin neat: its documented light fruit can disappear in a heavily flavored mix.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops before ice; neither independent review suggests needing heavy dilution.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained bitters and sweetness so the honey/apricot side stays clear.

Manhattan

A lighter vermouth and small trial pour suit its soft profile better than a heavily fortified mix.
