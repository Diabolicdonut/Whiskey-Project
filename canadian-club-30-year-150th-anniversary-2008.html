Background and history

The 2008 release marked the brand’s 150th anniversary. Contemporary coverage describes autumn distribution across Canada, the U.S. and selected duty-free markets.

This anniversary is the Canadian Club brand milestone, not the 2017 Dominion of Canada anniversary associated with the forty-year. The distinct event, strength and age keep the releases separate.

Critical review synthesis

John Hansell · Whisky Advocate

Hansell finds remarkable freshness, balanced sweet grain, fruit and spice, and polished restraint in the old oak. His 93-point judgment praises purity without claiming unlimited richness.

Scope: Summer 2009 review of the 150th Anniversary 30-year / 40% ABV release.

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The specialist finds lively fruit, pepper and creamy texture integrated with substantial dry wood and tobacco-like character. The ending persists, while complexity can be subtle and some bitterness remains.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start with a small neat pour and allow the subtler fruit/wood details time to appear.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops before ice because the positive records emphasize delicate polish at 40% ABV.

Old Fashioned

A restrained Manhattan-style serve can work, but avoid strong sweetening that hides subtle aged character.

Highball

If mixing, use a low soda ratio; the historical luxury bottle is most useful for tasting its own execution.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
