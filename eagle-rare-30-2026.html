What independent reviewers found

Release-matched published tasting synthesis, not a tasting performed by the dossier author.

Nose

Lifted cherry, cocoa, praline, bright herbal/melon accents and disciplined aged oak.

Palate

Rich chocolate, sweet dark fruit, mint, caramel and evolving spice, unexpectedly lively at 30 years.

Finish

Long mint/cinnamon/cherry echoes with some deep dry oak; several critics find the tannins restrained for the age.

Sources, distinctions and reservations

  • Drinkhacker — David Tao: Apr 27 2026 repeat matched tasting; layered cherry, spearmint, chocolate and controlled aged wood; strongly positive
  • Forbes — Tony Sachs: Apr 16 2026 personal sample: caramel, dark fruit, chocolate; dry lingering oak finish
  • Whisky Advocate — Julia Higgins: Apr 16 2026 preview sample: vanilla, apricot, brown sugar, raisin, and sustained mature finish

At the highest end of the Bourbon anchor scale, 9.5 reflects three convergent first-person reviews of remarkable depth without excessive wood; the finish remains a little dry. It is not automatically a 10 for age or reputation.

How to enjoy this specific release

Best: small neat pour in a nosing glass, rested 10 minutes. Start without ice so fruit, spice and finish can be compared with the evidence. Then add a few drops of water to test tannins and proof.

Rocks: one large cube only if heat distracts; dilution may blur the finish. Cocktails: expensive specialty releases are a poor default mixer at the original price; use an affordable high-proof alternative for an Old Fashioned or Manhattan.

Comparison pours: Eagle Rare 25, George T. Stagg, Michter’s 20 Year and other exceptional bourbons obtainable at a fraction of the price.
