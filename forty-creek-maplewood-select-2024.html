The short version

A corn-and-rye blend with sugar-maplewood influence. Matched accounts disagree on how convincing the sweet wood character is, so its approachable profile receives a cautious provisional assessment.

Exact identity: Forty Creek Maplewood Select, 2024 eighteenth Annual Limited Edition, 45% ABV / 90 proof, NAS; corn-and-rye blend treated with kiln-dried sugar maplewood.

Background and history

The producer announced this eighteenth annual limited edition on September 17, 2024, for Canadian availability from mid-September. Eighteenth identifies the series position, not eighteen-year-old whisky.

Launch context was CAD 89.95; the exact LCBO 39103 / 750 mL listing now shows CAD 90.20 when checked October 15, 2026. Neither amount is a verified U.S. offer. A similarly named Maplewood Reserve panel record is not silently merged with Select.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Campari’s September 2024 announcement authenticates corn and rye components and extraction of flavor from kiln-dried sugar maplewood. It does not specify component ages, the full oak maturation schedule, contact time or a syrup-seasoned cask. The exact producer photograph reads Maplewood Select, 45% and 750 mL.

Critical review synthesis

André · Québec Whisky

André finds an unconvincing sweet-fruit aroma, ordinary texture and a short ending despite enjoyable pepper and wood. Perceived artificiality is his sensory impression, not an ingredient finding.

Scope: June 4, 2025; explicitly the 2024 annual release, 45%.

The Gentleman’s Flavor tasting writer · The Gentleman’s Flavor

This independent annual selection finds easy, polished execution with familiar sweetness, spice and modest added character. It favors accessibility while wanting greater adventurousness.

Scope: Top Canadian Whiskies of 2024, exact Maplewood Select / 45%; annual-list opinion is not a population consensus.
