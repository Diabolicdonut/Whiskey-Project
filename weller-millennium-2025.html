Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The Weller name honors William Larue Weller and the brand’s historic association with wheated bourbon. Current Weller whiskey is made at Buffalo Trace in Frankfort, Kentucky. A historic connection to Stitzel-Weller is not evidence that a modern bottle contains old Stitzel-Weller stock. 2

Exact 2025 Millennium edition; straight-whiskey blend rather than straight bourbon. The editions are assessed separately. 3

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The second edition uses 2001, 2003 and 2006 vintages. The gold presentation placard distinguishes it from the original white placard. Reported vintage proportions are 2.9%, 46.5% and 50.6%; they are not an averaged mash bill or a guaranteed age statement. 3

Published percentages are reproduced only where disclosed. A distiller’s address, a retailer’s style field, an award entry and the actual bottle label answer different questions; unresolved differences are stated in the scope note rather than combined into a fictional recipe.

Vintage component percentages describe when whiskey was distilled, not how much corn or wheat is in the total recipe. Complete cask / tank storage history and bourbon-versus-wheat proportions are not published, so no average age or blended grain percentage is calculated.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Cherry, vanilla and mature sweetness in the identified second-edition account.

Palate

Caramel and settled oak at 99 proof. The surviving written accounts are shorter than those for the first edition.

Finish

A dry, wood-and-spice close; depth and intensity are not well enough triangulated to assert superiority over 2024.

Kirk Miller · InsideHook

Miller’s second-edition tasting identifies cherry, vanilla, caramel and wood spice, followed by dryness. This is direct tasting evidence, although a short account rather than an extensive comparative panel. 1

UPROXX

The second-edition tasting is more enthusiastic: ripe cherry, honey and vanilla lead to a buttery palate, then added spice across a sustained finish. It prefers this blend to the first edition for richer development. That positive texture judgment differs from the shorter, drier InsideHook account; no family ranking is converted into anchored Quality. 4

Where the judgments differ

The producer calls this an improvement, but the independently retrievable accounts do not establish a universal winner between editions. A separate dossier prevents the first blend’s notes becoming inherited consensus.

Consumer context

Consumer enthusiasm is context, not a representative quality panel. Availability, gifts and completing a family set can affect purchase motives, but do not add drinking-quality points. No sufficiently reliable exact-current-bottle consumer sample was used to override the identified critical evidence; comments about other proofs, batches or finishes are not pooled.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Direct reviewer or producer serving observations carry citations. Everything labeled editorial inference is practical reasoning from the documented format, not a performed tasting.

Neat, in a small glass

Published accounts evaluate these aged releases neat. A small pour lets aroma, wood and texture be assessed without making the expensive presentation the subject of the tasting.

A separate water trial

Editorial inference: compare a few drops in a second sample, especially if dry oak dominates. No new water test has been performed for this dossier.

Value of a sample

Editorial inference: a sensibly priced bar sample is a more proportionate first step than an untested luxury bottle. This is drinking guidance, not a market-price or investment forecast.

Mixed-drink value

Editorial inference: a cocktail with heavy syrup or vermouth can conceal subtle grain and old-oak detail. The recorded retail premium supplies no evidence that this bottle improves a mixed drink; assess it in a modest neat sample first.
