The short version

Slightly above average rye; good, selective mixing value.

Current-style Double Rye has a clear botanical signature, but flavor identity and exceptional quality are different things. Post-2018 reviews often find a light, ordinary neat presentation, while the mint/anise/spice combination remains useful in drinks. At 5.4 it sits just above the true rye midpoint. Its old blend’s mature Barton component is not allowed to inflate this assessment. 23

The current producer recipe combines MGP rye with High West’s own rye. It is 92 proof and at least two years old, with no chill filtration. The NC reference is the standard 750 mL bottle at $34.95, separate from higher-proof barrel selections. 14

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

High West built much of its identity around blending sourced and self-distilled whiskey. Double Rye’s composition changed in 2018 as High West’s own component replaced the older sourced component associated with earlier bottles. That is a material review boundary, not just new packaging. 2

The later blend can still be a distinctive choice. The important question is whether its freshness and botanicals compensate for reduced weight and maturity for a given use. A cocktail may reward that profile more readily than a slow neat comparison against a fuller rye.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The producer lists MGP’s 95% rye / 5% malted-barley recipe and High West’s 80% unmalted rye / 20% malted rye recipe. In the compact table those appear as R95 MB5 + R100: malted rye remains rye, not malted barley. No blended percentage is invented because the component ratio is undisclosed. 1

The current minimum is two years for both components. A review-era range extending higher is not a guarantee of the oldest whiskey in every current batch. Non-chill filtration is a confirmed process detail, but does not by itself establish body or complexity.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Mint, anise and herbal/botanical spice, sometimes quieter than expected.

Palate

Light honeyed sweetness with rye spice, tea-like herbal notes and modest weight.

Finish

Warm spice and botanicals persist, but development and concentration are limited in the dated post-change reviews.

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon · January 2021

Jordan Moskal’s 2021 review finds a serviceable standard rye with botanical personality but an ordinary, relatively forgettable presentation. It supports using current blend evidence rather than the old formula’s praise. 2

The Whiskey Wash · displayed byline Mark Littler · October 22, 2021

The 2021 review gives 5/10, describing a restrained nose and thin, honeyed/herbal palate. Cocktail usefulness does not erase the limited neat depth. 3

Rob Morton · Distiller · undated expert review

Rob Morton’s 89-point expert panel is considerably more favorable, finding developed mint/anise/spice and a long finish. Its tasting date is undisclosed, and updated recipe metadata does not prove when the liquid was reviewed, so it carries less weight for the post-2018 calibration. 5

Where the judgments differ

The botanical profile is broadly recognizable. The disagreement concerns weight, length and complexity, with an important date limitation on the strongest counterpoint. Two dated post-change accounts support a near-midpoint assessment; the favorable undated panel prevents a simplistic dismissal but does not establish excellent current quality.

Price & value

What the identified price buys

At $34.95 NC regular retail, Double Rye is good value for someone deliberately seeking bright botanicals. It is a weaker all-purpose neat bargain than Wild Turkey 101 Rye at $27.95 or Old Forester 100 Rye at $28.95. The producer’s current shop price is not substituted for the preferred NC benchmark. 4

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: High West directly recommends Manhattan and Old Fashioned use, and the dated reviews support a mixing role. Dilution details and other pairings are editorial inferences.

Neat / water

Start neat; the lighter body gives little reason for heavy dilution. A few drops can test whether botanicals become more expressive.

On ice

Use a large cube and drink before it becomes very dilute. Thinness is a documented limitation.

Old Fashioned

The producer recommends this serve. Restrain sugar so mint, anise and rye spice stay identifiable.

Manhattan

The producer specifically recommends a Double Rye Manhattan. A measured vermouth choice can complement its herbal profile.

Highball / cola

A chilled soda highball is a plausible botanical serve. Cola will bury much of the fresh herbal distinction.

Food / occasion

Most compelling as a mixer comparison or an accessible botanical-rye introduction rather than a premium neat showcase.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

The post-2018 blend is a rye midpoint reference. Breaking Bourbon finds an interesting herbal aroma but a light, average palate; the Whiskey Wash is similarly restrained, while the favorable undated Distiller tasting may describe an earlier blend. It is competent and free of the severe raw-oak failure in the provisional Hudson low sample, but lacks Redemption Ancients’ concentration and development. At $34.95, the current blend is fair approaching good value for a botanical style. Its light palate faces more developed rye at similar or lower prices, which narrows the previous good-value claim.
