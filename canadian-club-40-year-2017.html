The short version

A long-aged corn whisky praised for balanced sweetness and spice, with a useful counter-review noting thinness. Its all-corn identity is supported by expression-specific specialist accounts; later rye/Cognac additions are not transferred backward.

Exact identity: Canadian Club Aged 40 Years, 2017 Canada-only release at 45% ABV; original predecessor of the later named Chronicles blends.

Background and history

The producer places this Canada-only unveiling in 2017, marking Canada’s 150th anniversary. Specialist coverage records approximately 7,000 bottles and a later annual series using related stock.

The original forty-year preceded the named 41-year Water of Windsor and subsequent releases. Later website titles sometimes include it in a retrospective Chronicles catalogue; that does not make it the same blend as the later bottles.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

WhiskyCast identifies corn whisky distilled in 1977 and matured in barrels previously used for rye whisky. The dedicated Forbes release account independently reports a 100% corn base, with barrels consolidated over time. The original bottle is 45% ABV and forty years; added rye, Cognac or sherry described for later Chronicles releases is not inferred here.

Critical review synthesis

Mark Gillespie · WhiskyCast

Gillespie finds aromatic sweetness, honey balanced by spice and a sustained coherent ending. His 93-point November 2017 assessment supports excellent old-corn execution.

Scope: Original 1977-distilled / 2017-bottled forty-year, 45% ABV.

Coldorak · More Drams, Less Drama

The later tasting finds fruit and caramel developing with air, pleasing spice and an enduring finish, but less body than expected. It judges long aging successful without too much wood.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat and allow air; the later tasting reports fruit emerging over time.

Rocks / water

Use only a little water initially because one independent record finds limited body already.

Old Fashioned

Keep any sweetening or vermouth restrained to preserve the old corn-and-spice balance.

Highball

If using soda, keep the ratio small and compare with the neat pour before choosing it.
