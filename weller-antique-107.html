Background

Antique 107 is the higher-strength recurring Weller wheated bourbon. Its distinct buying case is sweet fruit, cinnamon and oily texture, with credible disagreement about heat and dryness; it should not inherit the reputation or tasting score of another Weller release.

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The Weller name honors William Larue Weller and the brand’s historic association with wheated bourbon. Current Weller whiskey is made at Buffalo Trace in Frankfort, Kentucky. A historic connection to Stitzel-Weller is not evidence that a modern bottle contains old Stitzel-Weller stock. 1

How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: cinnamon against fruit

Weller Antique 107 (107 proof): Allow the dark fruit and caramel to emerge before evaluating the spicy close. The reports of oily body and rowdy heat need to be judged together.

Water: drops for the rough edge

Weller Antique 107: A few drops may make fruit clearer at 107 proof. Stop if cinnamon and dry wood become dominant after the sweet middle thins.

Old Fashioned: spice as structure

Weller Antique 107: Keep syrup restrained and let bitters frame the cinnamon. Measured dilution should soften the heat while preserving the fuller wheated-bourbon texture.

Rocks: taste before dryness grows

Weller Antique 107: Use one large cube and follow the change from vanilla to wood. Cooling can make the drier side of some bottles more noticeable.

Critical synthesis

Critical synthesis

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Caramel, dark fruit, toasted grain and cinnamon, with more aromatic weight than Special Reserve.

Palate

A fuller, oily sweet-and-spicy structure. Some tasters find well-contained proof; others find rowdy cinnamon and alcohol.

Finish

Cinnamon, vanilla and mature wood persist; dryness can become more obvious after repeated sips.

Nick Beiter · Breaking Bourbon

Beiter finds a well-developed dark-fruit and cinnamon profile with satisfying texture and manageable strength. Its distinction comes from the balance of flavors, not just a larger proof number. 3

Alex · The Whiskey Shelf

Alex likes the sweet, spicy combination but reports more roughness, heat and dryness than the most enthusiastic account. His direct comparison favored an identified Maker’s Cask Strength bottle. 4

Bourbon Culture

In a semi-blind comparison, Antique shows cherry, cinnamon, chocolate and modest oak, but the tasted Maker’s Cask Strength is sweeter, fuller and preferred. Antique’s slightly thinner texture limits the claim of automatic superiority; this result belongs to the identified samples. 5

Where the judgments differ

Both major full reviews find a substantial step above Special Reserve, but they disagree about polish. That supports very good bourbon with bottle variation, rather than a blanket elite score.

Consumer context

Consumer enthusiasm is context, not a representative quality panel. Availability, gifts and completing a family set can affect purchase motives, but do not add drinking-quality points.
