Redemption Rye

The 95% rye mash gives Redemption a clear herbal identity. Its 92-proof standard bottle is friendly and useful, but competing budget ryes challenge its depth and value.

The short version

A high-rye recipe with a surprisingly easygoing delivery

Redemption Rye is a useful reminder that a high percentage of rye does not automatically produce a heavy, fiercely peppery whiskey. The standard 92-proof bottle often appears in reviews as lightly sweet, herbal and citrus-led, with mint and grain spice around a relatively slender center. It introduces the Indiana high-rye style without demanding a barrel-strength palate. 1 2 3

The main reservation is substance. Charles Steele’s 2023 review finds pleasant aromas and flavors but too little persistence or weight, while other reviewers respond more warmly to the same accessible profile. At almost $30 in NC, that difference has consequences: a whiskey can be agreeable and still struggle against fuller or stronger bottles at a similar price.

Background

A sourced rye helped bring the category back

Redemption belongs to the modern revival of rye whiskey, with sourced Indiana spirit central to its early identity. Independent reporting consistently identifies the distiller as MGP in Lawrenceburg. The brand later became part of Deutsch Family Wine & Spirits. That history is useful because Redemption is a brand and blending program, not evidence of a separate Redemption distillery making the base rye. 3

The range now includes bourbon, high-rye bourbon and other releases alongside the standard rye. Older reviews mention different age information and packaging. They can illuminate the family’s style, but they do not establish a current minimum age. This article concerns the current 92-proof rye, rather than a ten-year barrel-proof rye or a finished specialty release. 4

What is actually in the bottle?

The grain split is clear; the current age is less clear

The producer confirms 95% rye. Independent reviews identify the remaining 5% as malted barley, giving a widely corroborated 95/5 recipe. That makes Redemption substantially different from corn-rich Kentucky ryes such as Rittenhouse or Wild Turkey. Mint, floral or citrus impressions remain sensory tendencies, not guaranteed consequences of the numerical recipe. 4 2

The current producer specification gives 92 proof, 46% ABV, new charred oak maturation and non-chill filtration. The last point is worth checking directly: older third-party descriptions may say otherwise, but the current official product page explicitly identifies the process as non-chill-filtered. 4

The producer page does not give a numerical age or current minimum. Earlier reviews report young whiskey, sometimes with one- or two-year information attached to a particular bottle. Those historical labels do not justify assigning today’s bottle an exact two-year age, nor should the absence of an age on a webpage be converted into a legal minimum for the physical label. No secondary finish is disclosed for this standard expression.

Critical synthesis

A flavor profile, with the disagreements intact

These notes synthesize the named reviews below; they are not a first-person tasting of a newly opened bottle. Older reviews supply perspective on the standard expression, while current specifications come from current product sources.

Nose

Rye grain, mild herbs, mint, citrus and occasional honey or floral impressions. Aromatic intensity is often moderate.

Palate

Light caramel or honey sweetness, fruit, grain spice and young oak. One review adds toasted pecan and pastry notes.

Finish

Dry oak and spice recur, but length divides the accounts. The most critical review finds a quick, thin fade; others find lingering sweet herbs.

Wine Enthusiast · Kara Newman, 2022

Newman describes a relatively lean, dry rye with oak, spice and sweet barrel accents. The favorable assessment supports a clean, recognizable style rather than a particularly plush or richly textured whiskey. 1

The Whiskey Wash · Charles Steele, 2023

Steele finds faint grain, citrus and mint on the nose, followed by pecan, apple, pastry-like sweetness and young wood. His criticism concerns a watery delivery and brief finish: pleasant enough, but not much to linger over. 2

Secret Whiskey Society · Brad, 2024

This more positive account emphasizes honey, floral rye, bread, apple, peach, citrus and mint. Its sweeter, more persistent reading preserves a genuine disagreement with Steele about the satisfaction available from the standard bottle. 3

Where the judgments differ

The grain identity is easier to establish than the whiskey’s weight. A reviewer who values a fresh, easy rye may welcome the light structure. A reviewer seeking concentration hears a pleasant set of notes that disappears too quickly. The dossier gives greater weight to that structural limitation than to the marketing promise that a 95% rye recipe must be complex.

Price & value

What the current price buys

$29.95

NC ABC regular retail · 750 mL · August–October 2026 5

Reference checked October 4, 2026. Listing is not a local-store inventory guarantee; temporary monthly sale prices are excluded.

At $29.95 in NC, Redemption costs $2 more than Wild Turkey 101 Rye or Rittenhouse, and $1 more than Old Forester Rye. Each of those is bottled around 100 proof and offers a more forceful structural alternative. Redemption remains a reasonable purchase for its particular mint-and-citrus style, but its value is only good to fair when the whole budget-rye field is considered.

Best ways to enjoy

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: The producer supports straight drinking and classic cocktails, while Steele specifically criticizes brief, watery delivery. The individual drink adjustments are editorial inferences. 4 2

Neat / a few drops of water

Taste a small neat pour to find whether the mint, citrus and light sweetness appeal before making a judgment on weight. A little water can separate the herbs from the alcohol, but large dilution risks exaggerating the thinness identified by the more critical reviewer.

One large cube

Cooling can tame the young spice, but the reviewed thinness means melting ice can quickly leave a weak middle. A short pour over one large cube is preferable to heavy ongoing dilution.

Manhattan

Editorial inference: its mint and citrus provide a different accent from corn-rich rye. Keep vermouth and stirring restrained so the 92-proof, sometimes watery base does not disappear.

Whiskey Sour

Editorial inference: lemon can complement the citrus and sweet herbs. Moderate sweetener and dilution preserve rye identity; an oversized, very sweet sour emphasizes the bottle’s lightness.

A rye sour or a restrained Manhattan can make good use of its accessible high-rye character. Use care with a large dose of sweet vermouth or syrup if the aim is to keep the whiskey distinct. When a cocktail needs a more forceful base through heavy dilution, one of the 100-proof Kentucky ryes is the more convincing purchase.

Where it is less useful

Less ideal: heavily diluted highballs or richly sweet drinks where a fuller 100-proof budget rye would remain easier to taste.
