History and line identity

Freddie Noe’s second Little Book chapter, released in September 2018, blends eight-year Kentucky rye, thirteen-year Canadian rye and forty-year Canadian corn whisky at 118.8 proof. Forty years describes one component, not the age of the entire blend. The attraction is a sweet, aromatic cross-border whiskey whose honeyed middle meets persistent spice.

How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: compare fruit and spice

Little Book Chapter 2: "Noe Simple Task" is 118.8 proof. Allow the raisin and honey character to unfold before the warm finish. Judge the blend as a whole, rather than searching for the oldest component in isolation.

Water: soften the aromatic heat

A few drops can test whether the sweet floral middle becomes clearer. Stop before the honey-and-butterscotch texture loses its shape.

A restrained Manhattan

Try a small amount of sweet vermouth first. This blend already contributes considerable sweetness, so a vermouth-heavy ratio can cover the contrasting rye spice.

Avoid treating age as a mixer rule

The forty-year component does not forbid cocktails, but the point of this purchase is the blend’s development. Establish a neat reference before choosing sugar or ice.
