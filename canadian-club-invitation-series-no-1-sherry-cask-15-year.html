Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The November 15, 2023 producer release confirms a fifteen-year Classic-derived whisky with secondary aging in Oloroso sherry casks at 42% ABV. Stephen Beaumont identifies the corn and rye/malted-grain blend. The producer does not say how long the sherry stage lasts, so the three additional years relative to Classic 12 are not all assigned to that cask stage.

Critical review synthesis

Stephen Beaumont · Beaumont Drinks

Beaumont finds a nutty, fruit-filled aroma, assertive sweet entry, layered spice and a dry fruit-and-oak ending. He sees meaningful gains in richness and complexity from age and sherry treatment.

Scope: December 21 review associated with the 2023 launch; exact 42% ABV fifteen-year, producer-supplied sample disclosed.

Evan · Kensington Wine Market

The retailer’s own named tasting describes sweet confectionery and ripe fruit, followed by an easygoing spice finish. It finds a clear improvement over Classic 12; this is supplementary commercially interested tasting evidence.

Scope: Exact fifteen-year / 42% ABV entry; producer tasting copy is separately labeled and not treated as independent.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Begin neat with a small pour and allow time in the glass to evaluate wood, grain and fruit integration.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops of water before a cube; protect the documented texture and subtle mature flavors.

Old Fashioned

A small Manhattan or restrained Old Fashioned is possible, but taste neat before deciding to sweeten an expensive release.

Highball

Use a small measure of soda if desired; the old-stock premium does not automatically make it a better mixing whisky.
