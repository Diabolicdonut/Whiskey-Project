The short version

An unusual unmalted-barley single grain with real distillery character: fruity, nutty and sweet-spiced, sometimes dense and oily, sometimes lighter and brighter. The evidence supports good but not exceptional programme-level execution.

Exact identity: Shelter Point Montfort field-to-flask single-grain programme, represented by independently reviewed Lot 141 (2018 batch) and Lot 151 at 46%; lot differences are retained rather than collapsed into a fictitious invariant recipe.

Background and history

Montfort is named for Barrett Montfort and was conceived as a field-to-flask whisky from barley grown, distilled and bottled at Shelter Point.

Reviewed Lot 141 and Lot 151 show meaningful production and sensory variation. Older reviewed lots were triple-distilled; later producer material reports double distillation, so the dossier does not claim one invariant still regime.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Producer sheets and the independent lot comparison identify 100% unmalted barley and 46% ABV. Lot 141 and Lot 151 reviewed samples were triple-distilled in copper pot stills; later programme material reports double distillation. American and French oak appear in the documented lot programme, but exact proportions and ages remain undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Broddy Balfour · Dramface

Lot 141 is dense and oily with vanilla, clove, hazelnut, berries and chocolate; Lot 151 is brighter and lighter with honey, vanilla, citrus and fruit, with a shorter peppery finish.

Scope: Montfort Lot 141 2018 batch and Lot 151, both 46%, 100% unmalted barley.

Aengus · Dramface

Lot 141 is fruity, woody and nutty with baking spice and a slight craft note; Lot 151 is fresh and fruit-led with apple juice and cinnamon. Both are agreeable rather than exciting.

Scope: Second independent taster on the authenticated Lot 141 and Lot 151 comparison.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Neat is the best way to compare the dense Lot 141 style with the brighter Lot 151 character.

Rocks

A few drops of water are preferable to heavy ice if the oak or pepper feels prominent.

Highball

The bright fruit and grain profile can support a dry, lightly carbonated highball.

Old Fashioned

Use a very restrained sweetener to avoid masking the unusual unmalted-barley profile.

Manhattan

A lighter vermouth and modest pour are safer than a rich, sweet build.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
