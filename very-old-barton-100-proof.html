The short version

Good bourbon; exceptional drinking value at the state reference.

Very Old Barton 100 Proof illustrates the separation between Quality and Value. It brings recognizable fruit, caramel and assertive cinnamon, but can be thin, hot or dry at the edges. That is good bourbon at 6.2, not an elite whiskey because it is inexpensive. At Virginia ABC’s $16.99 regular 750 mL reference, the same profile becomes exceptional Value. 2341

The exact bottle matters. This dossier covers the no-age-statement, non-bonded 100-proof release. Memories of a six-year age statement or older Bottled-in-Bond designation should not become current specifications.

Background

This page concerns standard current 100-proof Very Old Barton, not an obsolete six-year guarantee. Its place is straightforward budget bourbon: cinnamon, caramel and fruit give it character, while body and dry heat keep it outside a premium tier.

The name, the era and the limits of the story

Very Old Barton is a long-standing budget label associated with Barton 1792 in Bardstown. Its branding can be easy to overlook beside more heavily promoted bourbon, and several proofs have existed. The 100-proof expression has a particular following because it offers enough strength and recognizable house flavor for neat use or mixing. 4

Current retail presence is verified, but the research did not establish a definitive producer statement about ongoing production of this exact bottling. Rumors of discontinuation therefore are not presented as fact. A state listing supports the price benchmark, not a promise that every store has stock.

Specifications without invented precision

The NAS straight-bourbon label supports at least four years, while a reported four-to-six-year blend is not promoted into a fixed age guarantee. The frequently cited 75/15/10 corn/rye/malted-barley recipe is also marked reported. The secure current identity is 100 proof Kentucky bourbon in a 750 mL bottle. 341

The lack of a bonded claim is meaningful even at 100 proof. Proof alone does not establish Bottled-in-Bond provenance. This bottle should likewise not be confused with NC’s listed 86-proof Very Old Barton; that different expression’s local price is not used here.

How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: cinnamon over polish

Very Old Barton 100 Proof (100 proof): Follow the caramel and orchard fruit into the dry cinnamon and pepper. The price explains its role, but cannot remove the occasional thinness or youthful alcohol.

Water: watch bitterness

Very Old Barton 100 Proof: ScotchNoob found increased bitterness with dilution. Test drops in a separate sample and stop if dry wood outlasts the fruit more sharply.

Old Fashioned: use the spice

Very Old Barton 100 Proof: At 100 proof the cinnamon and caramel can survive measured stirring. Use restrained syrup and bitters, then adjust after tasting the dry finish.

Rocks: retain some warmth

Very Old Barton 100 Proof: One large cube gives a gradual trial. Excessive cooling may suppress the fruit while leaving oak and pepper; taste early in the melt.

Critical synthesis

Critical synthesis

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Cinnamon, caramel and orchard fruit with some oak and noticeable alcohol.

Palate

Classic caramel and spice, occasional richer fruit, but inconsistent coating texture and some youthful heat.

Finish

Dry oak, pepper and cinnamon can persist. Water may increase bitterness rather than improve balance.

Mike & Mike · Bourbon Culture · 2021

The 6/10 review finds more than one-note budget sweetness, including fruit and darker tobacco/leather associations, but also heat and bitter dryness. It supports good rather than premium-level bourbon. 2

ScotchNoob · November 9, 2020

ScotchNoob finds an enjoyable dry structure and fruit/caramel character, yet notes thin body and alcohol. Dilution is not an automatic improvement; increased bitterness limits a simplistic “add water” recommendation. 3

Ed Escott · Bourbon Obsessed · January 24, 2022; updated June 19, 2023

Ed Escott enjoys the standard 100-proof bottle, especially its cinnamon-led profile and moderately long finish. His favorable account provides a third independent assessment without resurrecting the former six-year specification. 4

Where the judgments differ

The main split is how much dry spice and heat the drinker accepts. Richer fruit in one account and a cinnamon-dominant profile in another are compatible with batch or tasting differences. The recurring texture and rough-edge complaints keep the score near 6, while the distinct flavor prevents a merely average verdict.
