Background and history

Still Waters released Stalk & Barrel single malt in the early 2010s as one of the defining products of Ontario's modern microdistilling wave.

The 46% expression has appeared as single-cask and broader retail presentations. This dossier keeps the common proof/name together but does not pretend every numbered cask had identical wood treatment.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The 46% single malt is made from locally sourced two-row malted barley and pot-distilled at Still Waters. Later exact-expression records identify ex-bourbon maturation, while early numbered casks demonstrate that oak treatment varied across the programme. Finds halva, hay, pear and apple-pie aromas, then sweet fruit, cereal, ginger and cinnamon with a creamy medium finish; calls the three-year whisky already tasty while clearly anticipating improvement with age.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat to evaluate cereal, honey and fruit before deciding whether the young edges need water.

Rocks

A small cube can round grassy heat but may also mute the delicate orchard-fruit notes.

Highball

A clean soda highball suits the cereal and honey profile.

Old Fashioned

Use minimal sugar and orange oil; let malt sweetness do most of the work.

Manhattan

Possible, but use a lighter vermouth so the youthful malt is not buried.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
