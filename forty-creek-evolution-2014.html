The short version

A technically unusual historical release with strongly opposed sensory evidence. Its redistillation and Cabernet-cask accounts are attributed, and neither the process nor the bottle’s forty branding supplies a label age.

Exact identity: Forty Creek Evolution, 2014 Lot 2014 eighth annual limited release, 43% ABV / 86 proof, NAS; French-oak Cabernet Sauvignon influence.

Background and history

Contemporary WhiskyCast documents planned September 27–28, 2014 release weekend and approximately 9,000 numbered bottles. The exact archive photograph shows 2014 Evolution, not another annual bottle.

The label remains NAS. Dykstra’s page displays an April 2014 date, while the later May interview describes a coming autumn release; sample/review timing does not by itself establish retail availability. A dated, exact U.S. regular price was not authenticated.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

In a May 2014 direct interview, John Hall describes three years in American white oak, redistillation, then about nine years in his former Cabernet Sauvignon French-oak barrels, followed by other selected stocks in contemporary accounts. The current producer archive instead says half the blend spent five years in Cabernet barrels. These incompatible accounts remain attributed; no single exact whole-blend cask schedule is asserted. Redistillation also prevents simply adding the pre- and post-distillation periods into a final age statement.

Critical review synthesis

Blair Phillips · Distiller

Phillips finds substantial changing fruit, floral and savory detail, with an assertive pepper-and-tannin ending. His account supplies positive complexity evidence.

Scope: Professional exact Evolution tasting section, 43%, NAS; community ratings not used as a substitute.

Chris Martine · Whisky Magazine

Martine finds insufficient body for the dense oak, with prominent tannins and grain-spirit impressions. His sample is a direct challenge to an assumption of successful maturity.

Scope: Exact Evolution, year bottled 2014, 43%, explicitly NAS; accessible full taster section.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra finds initially appealing sweetness and spice undermined by intrusive sour, oxidized-wine and possible sulfur impressions. The specific defect evidence materially limits the score.

Scope: Exact 2014 Evolution, 43%; page shows April 10, 2014. Sensory impressions are not a laboratory finding or proof every bottle shares a fault.

Editorial interpretation of inspected independent samples, not first-person tasting. Conflicting sensory judgments and historical sample eras remain explicit. Publisher grades are not mechanically converted; ratings and producer descriptions alone are not sensory evidence.
