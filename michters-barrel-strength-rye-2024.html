The short version

Excellent rye; good premium value at original $110 MSRP.

The 2024 Michter’s Barrel Strength Rye release sits near 8 because strong independent evidence supports layered sweetness, herbs, spice and mature integration. Whisky Advocate’s 93-point Barrel 24B0481 and VinePair’s 94-point sample are persuasive, while The Whiskey Reviewer’s B+ adds a lighter, greener finish counterpoint. 134

This is a single-barrel product. The index displays 110 proof as the representative strength of Barrel 24B0481, not a fixed proof for every 2024 bottle. The producer’s release average is 110.2. A reader’s own barrel code and proof remain essential identifiers. 2

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The producer announced the 2024 bottling after the previous release in 2022, with a $110 suggested price for 750 mL. Barrel-strength rye is separate from Michter’s standard US*1 rye and its ten-year rye. A score for one of those lines is not evidence for this release. 2

The reviewed barrels are not fully matched. Whisky Advocate provides 24B0481; VinePair reviews a 110-proof sample without publishing a barrel code. The Whiskey Reviewer discusses another sample. Their common flavor strengths support a release-level assessment, while variation limits a universal barrel promise.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The February 2024 producer release identifies single-barrel bottling and 103 entry proof. Lower entry strength is a verified production detail for this release context, but does not reveal the exact grain recipe or maturation years. Both remain undisclosed rather than inferred from a flavor note. 2

The actual 110-proof barrel used as the index reference is 55% ABV. The 110.2 release average is 55.1%. Neither should replace the proof printed on another bottle. A displayed proof marked representative is more accurate than averaging all reviews into an invented exact-barrel dossier.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Chocolate/caramel sweetness, herbal rye and spice, with some savory or green accents.

Palate

Layered sweet oak, honey and cocoa over herbs/spice; texture can begin thin before becoming more substantial.

Finish

Honey, chocolate, earthy oak and rye spice. One barrel’s ending is lighter and more green-wood/astringent.

David Fleming · Whisky Advocate · Summer 2024

David Fleming rates Barrel 24B0481 93, finding cream/chocolate sweetness, mint/licorice and a long honeyed, spicy, earthy ending. The precise code and proof make this the representative barrel in the metadata. 1

David Thomas Tao · VinePair · April 10, 2024

David Thomas Tao gives a 110-proof sample 94. He finds deep, changing sweet/savory character, but notes initial texture thinness and wishes for more baking spice at the end. Its barrel code is not supplied. 3

Richard Thomas · The Whiskey Reviewer · May 2024

Richard Thomas gives B+, finding enjoyable herbal sweetness and cocoa but a lighter finish with green-wood dryness. This independent counterpoint matters when assessing a release made up of separate barrels. 4

Where the judgments differ

Much of the difference is plausibly barrel variation rather than conflicting observations of the same whiskey. The broader evidence supports excellent character, while lighter texture or green wood in individual accounts keeps the release below a confidently elite 9. It is not scored by averaging 93, 94 and B+.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: All serve recommendations are editorial inferences. The three critical sources assess flavor, and the release announcement establishes proof variation and production.

Neat / water

Use a small neat pour, then measured drops. Barrel strength varies, and a favorable water result from one bottle is not universal.

On ice

A large cube is an option, but may reduce the fine herbal/chocolate detail. Establish the neat baseline first.

Old Fashioned

Enough structure for the drink by inference, but keep sweetness restrained and compare the barrel’s own layers before mixing.

Manhattan

A premium rye Manhattan is plausible, though vermouth selection can overwhelm the distinctive sweet/savory balance.

Highball / cola

A costly use for a mixer that hides much of the excellent barrel-specific character.

Food / occasion

Best as a single-barrel comparison or slow rye tasting, with the actual barrel code recorded beside the glass.
