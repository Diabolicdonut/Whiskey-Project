Exact identity: Original 2020 Decadence, batch 1, bottle 1301 as reviewed by David Levine; 91 proof, maple-syrup-barrel finish. The photographed label has no numerical age statement.

Background and history

Decadence’s first 2020 batch establishes the maple-syrup-barrel branch of the family. The long-running finishing concept is represented here by its launch identity and by the already accepted 2025 review.

Ordinary intervening batches are not added merely to fill years. A changed recipe or finish, rather than a different handwritten number, is required for additional dossier separation.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The photographed first-batch bottle identifies a blend of straight bourbons finished in maple-syrup barrels at 45.5% ABV. Levine describes a ten-year base, but the label says aged to perfection without a numerical age. Source distilleries and grain proportions remain unconfirmed.

Critical review synthesis

David Levine · Whiskey in My Wedding Ring

Levine reports dense maple/brioche sweetness, substantial vanilla, a mineral counterpoint and an oily medium-to-full body. He regards the finish as sweet and well integrated rather than a finish that loses the bourbon base.

Scope: 2020 batch 1, bottle 1301, 91 proof.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat to assess the rich sweetness in the exact first-batch record.

Rocks / water

Try a small amount of water after the neat pour; its substantial sweetness can survive some dilution, but that is a trial rather than a promise.

Old Fashioned

Reduce added syrup because maple-cask sweetness is already the documented premise.

Manhattan

Use a restrained vermouth measure; extra sweet modifiers can overwhelm the bourbon/finish balance.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
