# Whiskey Dossier v6.2 — calibration hierarchy

Reviewed October 6, 2026 against the authoritative saved v6.1 collection. All existing supported numeric Quality and Value scores are preserved. The completed research, family expansion, pricing and native Bourbon/Rye recalibration are retained. Native anchor endpoints did not change; Rye’s previously investigated floor remains provisional.

## Quality calibration

1. **Native category anchors** when a defensible complete 1/5/10 framework exists.
2. **Parent-category anchoring** when the narrow category is sparse. Compare quality of execution, not likeness to the parent’s flavors.
3. **Universal whiskey-quality rubric** when no useful parent hierarchy exists.
4. **Evidence-limited provisional scoring** when thin independent evidence is nevertheless sufficient for a reasoned exact-expression estimate. The underlying parent, universal or native comparison is recorded.

**N/A is reserved for genuinely insufficient matched independent sensory evidence or materially uncertain exact identity. Missing category endpoints alone never require N/A.** A program overview is not one whiskey. A detailed review of a different proof, recipe, release year or barrel does not automatically identify the dossier’s target.

The derived **American straight-grain whiskey execution** parent framework borrows the established Bourbon defect/competence/reference examples: Cleveland Black Reserve early 2013–14 at 1; Maker’s Mark and Bulleit Bourbon at 5; Michter’s 20 Year 2024 at 10. It covers identified straight wheat, new-oak malt, corn and documented blends or finishes of such whiskey. Those references compare integration, development, finish, texture, appropriate maturity and clean execution. A corn or wheat whiskey need not acquire Bourbon flavors to score well. No new wheat, malt, corn or blend native endpoint is asserted, and the contested Rye floor is not used as a parent endpoint.

## Universal rubric

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Integration & balance | 25% |
| Flavor development & complexity | 20% |
| Finish quality & persistence | 15% |
| Technical cleanliness / absence of defects | 15% |
| Texture / body / mouthfeel | 10% |
| Maturity / wood management appropriate to style | 10% |
| Distinctiveness / successful execution of intended style | 5% |

The weighted result is a **diagnostic baseline**, not an automatic score. Each universal-based dossier records seven dimension judgments, the baseline, an editorial adjustment within ±0.5 and its reason, and the final rounded score. One-decimal rounding uses decimal half-up after adjustment. Unknown body, maturity or finish duration is explicitly cautious; a diagnostic midpoint is not an invented tasting observation. Decimal precision does not imply laboratory precision or a blind panel.

| Quality | Semantic meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Catastrophic |
| 2 | Extremely poor |
| 3 | Poor |
| 4 | Below average |
| 5 | Ordinary / competent |
| 6 | Good |
| 7 | Very good |
| 8 | Excellent |
| 9 | Elite |
| 10 | Reference-class |

These meanings apply to successful execution of the intended style. There is no target distribution or required occupancy of integer bands. Quality excludes price, prestige, scarcity and personal preference for a different style.

## Evidence and confidence

Substantive independent whiskey blogs, accessible YouTube transcripts or paired written tasting notes, independent competition tasting notes, specialist retailer editorial and detailed community tastings can support an assessment. Producer notes can establish intended style and specifications but are not independent sensory evidence. Mere awards, star-only grades, promotional copies, video titles and unexplained retailer notes do not establish a palate or finish. A channel’s written article and video are one voice; syndicated and reposted reviews count once. Published scores are neither averaged nor translated into this scale.

Confidence describes matching strength, narrative depth, independence and consequential disagreement. One substantial exact-expression account may support §. Two short accounts may still be provisional. A small unresolved strength discrepancy is explicitly disclosed; a different recipe or edition can require N/A. Strong repeated evidence does not remove identity limitations.

## Value calibration

When Quality and an identity-matched ordinary price/format are defensible, assess Value. Seek same-style competitors first, then useful parent-category competitors, then general whiskey buyer opportunity cost at the same budget. Cross-category Value comparisons answer a purchasing question and do not demand equivalent flavor profiles. Different intended uses are explicit for flavored whiskey.

Value is price-specific drinking utility, with 5 meaning fair. Rarity, packaging prestige, collector interest and resale prices provide no bonus. Historical release SRPs and out-of-stock dated listings are labeled references rather than current offers. Every cost comparison uses a 750 mL equivalent. A five-component Prohibition set price is not arbitrarily divided into invented individual-bottle prices. Rounded five-band purchase thresholds are editorial decisions around alternatives, not price forecasts or percentage formulas.

## Shared calibration metadata

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| quality_calibration_mode | native_anchored, parent_anchored, universal_rubric, evidence_limited_provisional or unscored |
| quality_calibration_basis | Specific execution framework and reasoning |
| quality_confidence | Evidence/matching confidence, or Unscored |
| value_calibration_mode | same_style_comparison, parent_category_comparison, buyer_opportunity_cost or unscored |
| value_comparison_basis | Ordered buying comparison, supported price context and exclusions |
| value_confidence | Evidence and price confidence, or Unscored |

The structured Quality details additionally identify underlying method, references and limitations. Universal assessments include their complete diagnostic rubric. Native anchor status remains separate: a category can have unestablished native endpoints and a numeric parent- or rubric-based expression score.
