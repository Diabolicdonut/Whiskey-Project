The short version

A genuinely distinctive smoky malt: salted caramel, iodine, graham cracker, tobacco and wood smoke over a dense sweet texture. The smoke comes from treated barrels, not peated malt, and independent reviews find the result integrated rather than gimmicky.

Exact identity: Shelter Point Smoke Point Single Malt in the current locally smoked-barrel regime, represented by Batch 3 at 53% ABV / 106 proof; older ex-Islay-cask Smoke Point and Smoke and Oak bottles are excluded.

Background and history

Shelter Point previously used ex-Islay casks for smoky releases. Independent review evidence explicitly says that line was discontinued before the locally smoked-barrel Smoke Point method.

The current producer describes a proprietary vatting aged in barrels smoked with shoreline driftwood and native woods, preserving the island-smoke identity documented in Batch 3.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The current producer identifies single malt matured in barrels smoked with local driftwood and native woods. Dramface’s exact Batch 3 review records 53% ABV and distinguishes it from the discontinued ex-Islay-cask regime; the SAQ independently lists Smoke Point Single Malt at 53%. No peat-malted-barley claim is made.

Critical review synthesis

Broddy Balfour · Dramface

Batch 3 is dense, thick and sticky, combining salted caramel, iodine, graham cracker, vanilla, pipe-tobacco and wet-wood smoke with a salty fruit-backed finish. The review finds the smoked-barrel method more integrated than the earlier ex-Islay approach.

Scope: Exact Smoke Point Batch 3, 53% ABV, current driftwood/native-wood smoked-barrel method.

Whisky Magazine tasting panel · Whisky Magazine

The 53% single malt shows apple, cracker, coconut, lemon, honey and fresh wood on the nose, then restrained sweet smoke with roasted fruit, jam, herbs and a long ginger-tea finish; the tasting calls it nicely put together.

Scope: Smoke Point Single Malt, 53% ABV, NAS; batch number not stated.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Neat is the priority; the dense texture and integrated smoke are the expression’s main strengths.

Rocks

A small cube can tame 53% ABV, but avoid over-diluting the long smoky finish.

Highball

A short soda highball will brighten salt and smoke while keeping the whisky recognizable.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained sugar and aromatic bitters so the barrel smoke stays central.

Manhattan

A drier, herbal vermouth can work with tobacco and smoke; keep the vermouth portion modest.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
