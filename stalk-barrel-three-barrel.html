The short version

An ambitious 43% blend with creamy malt, cocoa, citrus and warming spice in its best showing, but another exact review finds it hot, bitter and underdeveloped; the split evidence caps confidence and score.

Exact identity: Stalk & Barrel Three Barrel at 43% ABV / 86 proof, the 2020-era small-batch blend of separately ex-bourbon-aged rye, single malt and corn whisky. The 40% discovery seed is corrected.

Background and history

Three Barrel arrived around Still Waters' tenth-anniversary period in 2020 as a later blended-whisky development rather than a relabeling of Blue or Red.

Its name refers to the three whisky components/barrel streams in the blend, not to three sequential finishing casks.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Launch-era reporting by Canadian whisky writers Davin de Kergommeaux and Blair Phillips identifies Still Waters rye and single malt blended with traditional corn whisky, each component aged separately in ex-bourbon casks. Batches were made at under 1,000 litres. The exact reviewed expression is 43% ABV / 86 proof.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux & Blair Phillips · The Definitive Guide to Canadian Distilleries

Find creamy vanilla, barley sugar, chocolate, cocoa, citrus and warming clove/spice, presenting the blend as a successful step forward for the distillery.

Scope: May 23, 2020; exact Three Barrel launch-era whisky.

Megawatt · Whisky Connosr

Finds vanilla and cocoa on the nose but a hot, spirity palate that turns bitter and fades quickly, judging it disappointing and immature.

Scope: June 28, 2020; exact Three Barrel at 43% ABV.

The 5.8 score deliberately preserves the conflict between a positive specialist account and a negative full independent review. Neither is treated as universally representative of every batch.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Try neat first because the disagreement centers on integration, heat and bitterness.

Rocks

A small cube may improve the harsher showing, though over-dilution risks flattening the cocoa and spice.

Highball

A soda highball is a sensible fallback if the batch presents hot or bitter.

Old Fashioned

Use modest sugar; cocoa, vanilla and spice can make an interesting build if the sample is balanced.

Manhattan

Use a lighter vermouth and test a half-size build before committing a full pour.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
