The short version

A Canadian blend with a small, exceptionally old Scotch component, offering unusual fruit, wood and smoky/musty interplay. Reviewers disagree about sweetness, body and finish length.

Exact identity: J.P. Wiser’s Union 52, original British Columbia exclusive released November 2016 and reviewed through 2017, NAS whole blend, 45% ABV / 90 proof, 750 mL.

Background and history

The November 14, 2016 TWS tour report describes the initial November release, and Whisky Advocate reviewed it in Winter 2016. The provisional 2017 discovery era is corrected to 2016 without changing the immutable ledger id/order or filename.

British Columbia-exclusive distribution and the Scotch accent are documented. Canadian-blend calibration remains appropriate; neither Scotch single-malt nor American rye endpoints are imported.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Livermore’s published Binny’s interview confirms blending fifty-two-year Scotch into Union 52 at 4%. Contemporary reports identify a fifteen-year Canadian base, while the initial tour report alternates fifteen and sixteen years; NAS is retained for the whole pack. The Scotch distillery is not authenticated. Virgin-oak treatment of the Canadian component is reported, but a complete cask history is not established. This is blending of liquids, not proof of a Scotch-cask finish, and the entire bottle is not fifty-two years old.

Critical review synthesis

Toronto Whisky Society members · Toronto Whisky Society

The group finds added depth in the unusual blend, but accounts diverge: Bryan enjoys persistent smoke while Kyle does not perceive it. Xile describes a relatively short, drying end; Boyd finds thinness.

Scope: February 15, 2017 purchased-bottle group; Devoz’s repeated tour tasting is not counted as a new independent sample.

Mark Bylok · Whisky Buzz

Bylok finds substantial oily body and developing old-wood character after time in the glass, but sweetness can overwhelm the palate. Resting improves it; he does not find peat as clearly as some reviewers.

Scope: January 30, 2017 purchased bottle; opposes the thin-texture report rather than being silently averaged away.

Davin de Kergommeaux · Whisky Advocate

The contemporary account is more favorable about pronounced smoke and a lush mouthfeel, supported by fruit and cereal character.

Scope: Winter 2016 exact 45% release; no automatic conversion of the publication’s grade.
