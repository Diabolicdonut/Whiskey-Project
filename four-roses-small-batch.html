Four Roses Small Batch

Fruit, floral detail and baking spice give Four Roses Small Batch a distinctive balance at 90 proof. It rewards attention more than a search for sheer intensity.

The short version

Four recipes, one deliberately gentle bourbon

Four Roses Small Batch is an appealing bourbon for a drinker who wants aromatic range without high proof. Reviewers return to red fruit, caramel, floral or herbal detail, oak and baking spice. The four-recipe blend supplies a real production distinction, while the 90-proof strength keeps the flavors in a relatively gentle frame. 1 2 3

The gentleness can be its weakness. Breaking Bourbon finds the palate a little timid despite an attractive longer finish; Bourbon Culture notes some green wood and residual ethanol; Phil Dwyer’s newer review is considerably more enthusiastic about texture and dessert-like sweetness. The dossier recognizes a very-good bourbon with useful nuance, without converting every favorable note into a claim of exceptional density. 2 3 4

Background

The regular blend is distinct from Select and limited releases

Small Batch belongs to the brand’s modern American revival. Kirin acquired Four Roses in 2002 and restored Kentucky straight bourbon to a domestic market that had previously known a different blended whiskey under the name. Single Barrel followed in 2004 and Small Batch in 2006, under Jim Rutledge. Brent Elliott became master distiller in 2015. That sequence explains why a long-established brand can have a comparatively young regular expression. 5

Ownership changed again in 2026: Gallo completed its purchase from Kirin on April 2. Its announcement retained Elliott and the existing team, and described continuity in production. The acquisition updates the corporate record; it does not by itself establish a new Small Batch recipe or invalidate an older tasting of the standard bourbon. 6

Four Roses built its modern identity around multiple recipes created from two mash bills and five yeast strains. Small Batch uses four of those recipes, rather than representing a single barrel or one recipe alone. Its continuing role in the core range makes it a useful bridge between the lower-proof entry bourbon and the more assertive single-barrel offerings. 1 2

The name requires care because Small Batch Select is a separate 104-proof expression, and Limited Edition Small Batch releases have their own ages, recipes and strengths. Neither belongs in the regular bottle’s tasting or price record. This article covers standard Small Batch at 90 proof, with the producer currently describing six-to-seven-year maturation. 1

What is actually in the bottle?

Two mash bills and two yeast styles, without a fake average

The four recipes are OBSK, OBSO, OESK and OESO. The B mash is reported as 60% corn, 35% rye and 5% malted barley; the E mash as 75% corn, 20% rye and 5% malted barley. K supplies a spice-focused yeast profile and O a rich-fruit profile. The producer confirms the recipe identities, but exact blend percentages are not established here, so no averaged mash bill is invented. 1 2 3

The producer specifies 90 proof, or 45% ABV, and aging of six to seven years. The bottle does not carry a conventional numerical age statement, so the producer’s description is recorded separately from label NAS status. This is batched Kentucky straight bourbon matured in new charred oak, with no disclosed secondary finish. 1

Small batch is a producer’s selection description, not a fixed legal barrel count. A reviewer’s estimate or a number attached to one blend should not be assumed to apply forever. The whiskey is fixed-proof, not cask strength or Bottled-in-Bond, and it should not acquire Small Batch Select’s filtration or proof specification through a shared name.

Critical synthesis

A flavor profile, with the disagreements intact

These notes synthesize the named reviews below; they are not a first-person tasting of a newly opened bottle. Older reviews supply perspective on the standard expression, while current specifications come from current product sources.

Nose

Caramel, red fruit, citrus, gentle oak and baking spice. Some accounts add floral or buttery pastry impressions.

Palate

Fruit and sweetness meet a restrained rye-spice structure. Texture is often creamy, but intensity ranges from delicate to notably satisfying.

Finish

Oak, fruit, caramel and warm spice remain. The finish can have greater range than the palate, though youthful wood or ethanol appears in one account.

Breaking Bourbon · 2017

The review finds caramel, citrus, fruit and oak with a creamy but delicate palate. Its most favorable observation is the longer, more dimensional finish, while the middle could use greater impact. 2

Bourbon Culture · 2020

This account finds brown sugar, red fruit, cinnamon, mint and moderate sweetness with more body than expected at 90 proof. Green oak and a little ethanol keep the judgment qualified rather than unreserved. 3

The Whiskey Wash · Phil Dwyer, 2025

Dwyer’s enthusiastic tasting emphasizes butter, cookie dough, caramel, cherry, mint and warm spices. Its silky, richly sweet reading offers a favorable counterpoint to the older review’s description of timid delivery. 4

Where the judgments differ

All three reviews describe a bourbon that favors balance over aggression, but they value its gentle frame differently. To one, the soft delivery makes the fruit and spice appealing; to another, it leaves the middle too timid. A multiple-recipe blend can create range without making every sip feel dense, and the number of recipes should not substitute for judging the actual integration.

Best ways to enjoy

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Producer recipes and Dwyer’s professional commentary support stirred and sour uses. Detailed ice and modifier adjustments are editorial guidance drawn from the reviewed gentle frame. 1 4

Neat / a few drops of water

Try a neat pour and give the finish time to develop. The bottle’s main interest is the way fruit, caramel, oak and spice share space, rather than a single dominant note. Avoid heavy dilution initially, since the palate is already gentle in some accounts.

One large cube

Cold can make a creamy gentle pour appealing, but dilution can obscure delicate fruit and floral detail. Use one large cube and monitor the flavor before the finish fades.

Old Fashioned

The producer and Dwyer identify it as useful here. Editorially, restrained sugar and bitters leave room for red fruit and baking spice rather than burying the four-recipe balance.

Whiskey Sour / Gold Rush

Dwyer specifically discusses sour and honey-citrus applications. Editorially, keep lemon and honey restrained so the gentle 90-proof base contributes more than generic sweetness.

Its approachable strength makes it a useful guest bourbon and a good choice for a restrained Old Fashioned, sour or honey-and-citrus cocktail. A drinker who likes the aromatic direction but wants more force can explore a stronger Four Roses expression separately. Judge that upgrade by its own recipe and proof, not by assuming that Select is merely this bottle with less water.

Where it is less useful

Less ideal: heavily bitter or over-diluted drinks that erase the fruit-and-floral detail; a stronger Four Roses expression may suit those roles better.

It sits near Elijah Craig, below Knob Creek 9’s maturity, and lacks the concentration and evolving finish of the Michter’s ceiling. Barrel-strength Four Roses releases are separate evidence.
