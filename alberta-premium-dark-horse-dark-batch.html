The short version

An inventive, approachable Canadian blend whose wine addition adds fruit and sweetness. Reviews agree on interesting flavors but disagree on body and finish length.

Exact identity: Alberta Premium Dark Horse in Canada / Alberta Rye Dark Batch in the U.S.; the documented 45% ABV rye-whisky, bourbon and sherry blend.

Background and history

Dark Horse was documented in Canada in 2012. The producer dates the renamed U.S. Dark Batch launch to April 2, 2015; this is one expression rather than two separate dossiers.

This historical release belongs to Alberta’s modern blending experiments. Its documented recipe distinguishes it from the family’s all-rye core and cask-strength bottles. A precise final production year is not authenticated here.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer confirms the 91/8/1 blend and 90 proof. Distiller reports six-year lower-proof pot-distilled rye in new oak and twelve-year higher-proof column-distilled rye in used bourbon wood. Those are component descriptions, not a twelve-year bottle age. The bourbon is identified as Old Grand-Dad by independent records.

Critical review synthesis

Distiller Staff · Distiller

The panel finds wine-led aromatics, fruit, oak and layered baking spice, with a gentle sustained ending. It rates the sample positively at 89 points.

Scope: U.S. Dark Batch, 45% ABV.

Bobby · Adventures in Whiskey

Bobby likes the wine and nutty sweetness but finds the texture light and the ending brief. The flavors become more compelling on a second tasting, earning a recommendation at the launch budget.

Scope: June 4, 2015 review of a producer-provided Dark Batch sample.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Try neat to assess wine, grain spice and the actual finish before sweetening.

Rocks / water

A large cube is a useful option; heavy dilution may emphasize the reported light body.

Old Fashioned

Start with little syrup because the blend already contains wine and documented sweetness.

Highball

The producer’s ginger-beer serve suits the blend; use bitters and a small orange squeeze.
