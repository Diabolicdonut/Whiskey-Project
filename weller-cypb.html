Background

C.Y.P.B. translates the producer’s crowdsourced design into an eight-year, 95-proof wheated bourbon. The retained articles assess the liquid independently of that story: balance and length recur, while enthusiasm for depth varies by release and writer.

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The Weller name honors William Larue Weller and the brand’s historic association with wheated bourbon. Current Weller whiskey is made at Buffalo Trace in Frankfort, Kentucky. A historic connection to Stitzel-Weller is not evidence that a modern bottle contains old Stitzel-Weller stock. 1

How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: construction over spectacle

Weller C.Y.P.B. (95 proof): Follow the caramel, fruit and baked-goods notes through the gentle nutty finish. An integrated sip need not be unusually vivid or floral.

Water: only if warmth distracts

Weller C.Y.P.B.: At 95 proof, start without water. Use drops in a comparison sample and stop when the full texture or baking aroma weakens.

Old Fashioned: understated sweetness

Weller C.Y.P.B.: A small amount of syrup and modest bitters preserve the balanced fruit and wood. Heavy sweetness makes the already restrained flavor arc harder to judge.

A Weller comparison

Weller C.Y.P.B.: Compare a small neat pour with Antique or 12 Year. The useful distinction is spicy intensity versus gentle mature balance, rather than the crowdsourced label.

Critical synthesis

Critical synthesis

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Sweet caramel, fruit and baking aromas, with the floral character varying between reviewed releases.

Palate

Well-integrated sweetness and oak at 95 proof; texture can be full even when the flavor arc is restrained.

Finish

Baking spice, wood and gentle nutty sweetness. Length is a strength in the introductory reviews.

Nick Beiter · Breaking Bourbon

The 2019 bottle is carefully balanced and enjoyable, but not the most exciting Weller. The author values construction while preferring Antique and 12 Year overall. 3

Bourbon Culture

The inaugural bottle receives a more enthusiastic assessment for fruit, maple-like sweetness and developed wood. It is a distinct older batch, not proof that every annual C.Y.P.B. performs the same way. 4

Matt Leonard · ABV Network

Leonard reports full texture and a long baked-goods finish, but misses the floral Weller signature. His total includes a price component, so it is not converted to this Quality scale. 5

Where the judgments differ

Balance is the strongest common claim. Enthusiasm for depth differs substantially across releases and writers; the crowdsourced story does not settle that disagreement.

Consumer context

Consumer enthusiasm is context, not a representative quality panel. Availability, gifts and completing a family set can affect purchase motives, but do not add drinking-quality points. No sufficiently reliable exact-current-bottle consumer sample was used to override the identified critical evidence; comments about other proofs, batches or finishes are not pooled.
