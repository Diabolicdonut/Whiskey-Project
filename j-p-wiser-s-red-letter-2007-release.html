The short version

A rich, powerful original Red Letter with mature sweetness, pepper and fresh-oak energy. The same evidence that praises its finish also records substantial heat and tannin.

Exact identity: Wiser’s Red Letter 150th Anniversary Edition, 2007 distillery-anniversary release, NAS verified pack, 45% ABV / 90 proof, 750 mL; original wooden presentation case.

Background and history

Released in 2007 for 150 years since the Wiser distillery’s 1857 beginning. It is a distillery anniversary, separate from Canada’s 2017 One Fifty commemoration.

The original wooden case and 150th Anniversary pack distinguish it from 2013 and 2025 editions. Later generic Red Letter reviews are excluded from this assessment.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Dykstra’s November 11, 2009 original-edition review reports direct Corby confirmation that all component whiskies were at least ten years old, followed by 150 days in virgin white oak, bottled at 45% without chill filtration. That source report is not an eighteen-year label. The photographed original pack shows no eighteen-year statement; the discovery age is corrected to NAS. A claim of being Canada’s only unfiltered whisky is era-limited promotional wording, not adopted as a verified market fact.

Critical review synthesis

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra praises oily richness and an energetic, lingering vanilla/spice finish, while explicitly noting heavy heat and fresh-oak tannin.

Scope: November 11, 2009 original 150th edition, including numbered original bottles; packaging grade excluded.

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The original-edition account finds complex fruit, wood and rye-like spice with a creamy palate and a long pepper-to-citrus ending. Hot mint and rubber-like notes remain part of the character, not a laboratory defect claim.

Scope: November 9, 2011 page dateline, exact 2007 anniversary edition; later release reviews excluded.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Rest a small neat pour to assess fruit, spice, texture and the drying finish in the published accounts.

Water / rocks

Add drops gradually or one small cube, comparing aroma and texture as it dilutes.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and bitters. A recurring Canadian blend is the cost comparison before mixing a limited bottle.

Manhattan / highball

Try a small serve with restrained vermouth or soda; a softer whisky can lose definition under assertive mixers.
