The short version

An affordable eight-year Canadian blend whose fuller sweetness does not guarantee refinement. Some specialists enjoy the fruit and creaminess; the September 2026 account finds conspicuous heat and a short finish.

Exact identity: Seagram’s VO Gold eight-year Canadian blended whisky, 40% ABV / 80 proof; distinct from regular modern VO and historical 86.8-proof VO.

Background and history

A 2019 first-person industry history describes Gold as an upmarket answer to VO’s early-1990s decline and interviews people involved in its creation. That places its development in that period without inventing an exact launch day.

The Seagram name predates the current owner. The 2018 sale announcement and Sazerac’s subsequent account establish the ownership transition; neither proves that every modern component was distilled at a particular plant. Historical Valleyfield accounts are kept in their own era.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Distiller

Finds creamy grain sweetness and fruit with peppery accents; the finish closes quickly with drying citrus-pith and oak-tannin impressions. Fuller than basic VO, but not a long or deeply developed pour.

Scope: Exact VO Gold / 40%; older undated expert entry, not the community star average.

Dave Broom · Whisky Magazine

Describes a grain-forward, initially aggressive aroma, a plain opening that develops some sweetness and fruit, and a clean short close. Finds it agreeable but insufficiently distinctive.

Scope: Exact eight-year / 40% entry; one visible reviewer tab, not two independently inspected accounts.

Patrick · Québec Whisky

Finds sweetness, pepper, nuts and charred wood, with alcohol that feels stronger than the stated strength. The finish is brief apart from lingering burn.

Scope: September 28, 2026, exact eight-year / 40% account; current dissent retained.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Assess neat first; recent evidence warns that low bottling strength does not eliminate heat.

Rocks

Try one small cube if the alcohol seems prominent; avoid flattening the short finish.

Old Fashioned

Use a restrained sugar measure with the already sweet blend.

Manhattan

A small 2:1 build is a reasonable starting point; dry vermouth can counter its sweetness.

Highball

A short ginger-ale highball is appropriate if the neat sample seems hot.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
