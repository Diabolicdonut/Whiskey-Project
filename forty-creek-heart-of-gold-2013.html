The short version

A floral, grain-led Canadian rye expression with strong independent support for distinctive development. It remains a blend with corn and barley, and the lot number is not a distillation year.

Exact identity: Forty Creek Heart of Gold, 2013 Lot 1972 seventh annual limited release, 43% ABV / 86 proof, NAS; documented rye-forward blend.

Background and history

The producer’s original release notice schedules September 28, 2013 availability and 9,000 numbered bottles. Lot 1972 is part of the product’s identifying presentation, not proof of a 1972 vintage.

The original Ontario price is CAD 69.95 / 750 mL. U.S. delivery-menu and collector listings do not provide a sufficiently reliable exact date/availability reference for numeric Value.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The contemporaneous producer announcement describes wine yeast, copper pot distillation with a narrower heart cut, lightly toasted oak, and rye supported by corn and barley. The current archive instead describes selected five-year whisky with a small in-house Gewurztraminer-brandy component. That additional recipe/age account is recorded as an unresolved retrospective claim, not used to invent complete-blend age, component proportions or a brandy-cask finish. The clear original rye-forward intent supports Canadian Rye Whisky editorial classification.

Critical review synthesis

Blair Phillips · Distiller

Phillips finds a coherent floral rye thread carrying a developed blend with supporting texture and controlled oak, rather than a merely sweet family variant.

Scope: Professional exact Heart of Gold tasting section at 43%; grain proportions not inferred from flavor.

Jason Hambrey · Whisky Connosr

Hambrey finds unusual grain and savory complexity, a less sweet palate than other family releases, and a particularly persistent ending. The sharp grassy character softens with time in the glass.

Scope: January 19, 2015; exact 2013 Heart of Gold, 43%; one substantive community sample, no aggregate consensus claimed.
