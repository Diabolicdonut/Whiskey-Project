The short version

A creamy, distinctive wood experiment with sharply divided reviews. Impressive texture and spice coexist with reported solvent-like aromas, bitterness and a fading finish.

Exact identity: J.P. Wiser’s Seasoned Oak, Rare Cask Series 2018, nineteen years, 48% ABV / 96 proof, 750 mL; Ontario limited release.

Background and history

The 2018 Rare Cask release was offered through Ontario LCBO and the distillery at approximately CAD 100. It is a separate expression from the simultaneously announced Canada 2018.

A distinctive cask treatment supports inclusion, but neither nineteen years nor an unusual cooperage process automatically improves execution.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Corby’s June 25, 2018 announcement verifies nineteen years and 48%, with a finish in new white-oak casks made from staves seasoned outdoors for over four years. Matched specialist reporting describes eighteen years in used cooperage, followed by approximately one year together in the seasoned new oak. Four years describes wood preparation before cooperage, not four additional years of whisky aging. No grain ratio is established.

Critical review synthesis

Selfbuilt · Selfbuilt’s Whisky Analysis

The sample has a complex aromatic opening and silky body, but solvent-like smells, excessive sweetness and lingering bitterness undermine it. Palate development and finish disappoint relative to the nose.

Scope: September 3, 2018, sample from Jason of In Search of Elegance; aromatic descriptors do not establish chemical contamination.

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The favorable account finds unusual fruit and wood character, creamy texture and a long spicy ending. This differs materially from Selfbuilt’s bitter, fading finish.

Scope: June 17, 2018 article dateline; article attribution retained separately from the site’s later migration metadata.

New Bourbon Drinker · New Bourbon Drinker

The purchased bottle provides intriguing oily texture but increasing heat and an early loss of flavor. The reviewer finds it interesting despite balance concerns and says it improves after opening.

Scope: May 5, 2019 exact nineteen-year release; original grade is not mechanically converted.
