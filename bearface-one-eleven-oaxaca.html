The short version

An adventurous whisky-and-mezcal blend with assertive smoke. Multiple reviewers find useful cocktail character, but thinness and intrusive smoke keep the assessment close to the good threshold.

Exact identity: Bearface One Eleven Series Oaxaca Agave Spirit, Batch 01, 42.5% ABV; ten parts Canadian whisky to one part Mexican mezcal.

Background and history

The 2019 Oaxaca release followed the 2018 Triple Oak launch and used Canadian blending flexibility to make an openly disclosed cross-spirit expression.

The producer still documents it as a limited release. A definitive discontinuation date or broad current U.S. availability is not established, so the discovery ledger’s unqualified discontinued status is narrowed to limited/historical.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The brand confirms the ten-to-one whisky/mezcal blend and names its mezcal maker. Original launch coverage describes virgin French-oak finishing of the Canadian component and two-year ex-bourbon maturation of the mezcal. The final bottle carries no authenticated age statement; the core Triple Oak’s seven years are not transferred.

Critical review synthesis

Mark Bylok · Whisky Buzz

Bylok finds forceful smoky-citrus contrast and substantial palate weight, with the acid helping the sweet whisky. He particularly enjoys it in cocktails and notes that its intensity may divide drinkers.

Scope: December 17, 2019 Batch 01; supplied bottle disclosed.

Aengus McCloud · Dramface

McCloud describes a growing rubber-like aroma, dilute texture and a moderately persistent bittersweet ending. He likes the idea more than the execution and favors the less costly core expression for most buyers.

Scope: March 2022, 42.5% ABV Oaxaca sample.

The Whiskey Wash tasting record; republished by Beth Squires · The Whiskey Wash

The tasting finds agave-led sweetness, caramel and smoke. Smoke becomes harsh on the initial sip before settling; the record judges the experiment largely successful and promising for mixed drinks.

Scope: June 2025 republication, 85-proof Oaxaca; current byline does not establish a fresh batch tasting.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Use a small pour and allow several sips to test whether the smoke remains pleasant.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops before a large cube; one critic already found the body dilute.

Old Fashioned

Reduce syrup and choose bitters that work with both agave and caramel.

Highball

Soda with citrus is a useful way to explore the smoky blend without hiding it.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
