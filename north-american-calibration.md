# North American expansion: category and calibration boundaries

Research opened October 14, 2026. This file supplements the accepted hierarchy without changing any of the 825 baseline dossiers.

## Canadian categories

| Editorial category | Identity boundary |
|---|---|
| Canadian Blended Whisky | Documented Canadian blends; the traditional word rye alone does not establish rye grain percentage. |
| Canadian Rye Whisky | Documented rye-forward composition or intended style; not automatically an American straight rye. |
| Canadian Single Grain / 100% Rye | Authenticated all-rye recipes, including malted/unmalted rye where documented. Distillation and oak regimes remain expression-specific. |
| Canadian Single Grain | Other authenticated single-grain whisky, such as Bearface's corn-based Triple Oak. |
| Canadian Single Malt | Authenticated single-distillery malt identity; no automatic comparison with American single malt or Scotch. |
| Flavored whiskey | Added flavorings/infusions are stated explicitly; a 35% ABV flavored product is not silently described as an unmodified Canadian whisky. |

These are editorial comparison groups, not invented Canadian legal standards. Legacy category text in the original corpus is preserved.

## Reference research and scoring gates

Native anchors require a defensible full low/mid/high framework. The discovery corpus contains useful practical references—entry blends, everyday rye, well-reviewed mature releases and craft malt—but evidence that an expression is less exciting does not establish it as a catastrophic 1, and an award does not establish a reference-class 10. No Canadian 1/5/10 endpoints are declared at discovery.

Canadian Club 1858 provides a practical entry-blend review reference: Stephanie Moreno finds a sweet profile given dimension by rye, best suited to ice or mixing. Lot No. 40 and Alberta's all-rye series provide different production and critical reference points; they are not borrowed Bourbon endpoints. The final expansion record will list the low, midpoint and high practical references actually researched and explain whether a complete native framework became defensible. [Canadian Club 1858, Distiller](https://distiller.com/spirits/canadian-club-1858)

Until a native framework is supported, Canadian assessments use the established universal execution rubric. A sufficiently useful Canadian parent framework can later support parent anchoring; unrelated American Bourbon or straight-rye endpoints are not used merely because the flavors overlap. One substantive matched independent review may support an evidence-limited provisional judgment; absence of endpoints alone does not require N/A. Producer notes, an award name without sensory detail, uninspected video titles and sibling reviews do not qualify as matched independent tasting evidence.

| Universal dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Integration and balance | 25% |
| Flavor development and complexity | 20% |
| Finish quality and persistence | 15% |
| Technical cleanliness | 15% |
| Texture and body | 10% |
| Appropriate maturity and wood management | 10% |
| Distinctiveness and successful intended execution | 5% |

Each universal-based numeric record carries seven cautious editorial dimension judgments, the weighted diagnostic result, an adjustment within ±0.5 and a reason. This is interpretation of attributed published evidence, not a new tasting panel. Thin evidence is labeled provisional; genuinely insufficient identity or sensory evidence stays N/A.

Quality retains the full spectrum with 5 as average/competent. Value uses a dated regular price and meaningful purchasing peers in the stated currency and market. Canadian-dollar references are not disguised as current U.S. or NC prices. Overall is √(Quality × Value), displayed to one decimal and sorted at full precision.
