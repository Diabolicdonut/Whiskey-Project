The short version

A competent cocktail-oriented Canadian blend with vanilla, nuts, fruit and light rye spice, but too much youthful bite and too little depth to become a convincing neat pour.

Exact identity: Stalk & Barrel Blue Blend at 40% ABV / 80 proof, the lower-priced companion to Red Blend and successor-era companion to the retired 1+11 blend.

Background and history

Blue and Red replaced the earlier 1+11 blended-whisky position in the Stalk & Barrel range, with Blue intended as the more accessible mixing whisky.

Blue should not inherit the higher malt/rye concentration or 43% strength of Red merely because the labels share a design.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

Independent and specialist records identify Blue as a blend of corn, rye and malt whiskies bottled at 40% ABV. It was deliberately positioned as the value/mixing member of the Blue/Red pair. Exact component ages and ratios are undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Finds rye, corn, vanilla, almond and fruit but persistent youth, sharp alcohol and limited depth; recommends it mainly as a mixer.

Scope: September 16, 2017; exact Blue Blend at 40% ABV.

Blair Phillips · Distiller

Treats Blue as a skillfully blended mixer, emphasizing vanilla, almond, fruit grain, mild rye spice and a toasty finish.

Scope: Exact Blue Blend editorial tasting at 40% ABV.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Useful for checking the vanilla, almond and grain profile, but the reviews do not favor neat as its best role.

Rocks

Ice softens the youthful bite; keep dilution modest.

Highball

A strong use case: soda or ginger ale gives the blend space without demanding much depth.

Old Fashioned

Keep the build simple and light on sugar; this is more mixer than contemplative base.

Manhattan

Not the first choice for a Manhattan; a richer blend or rye will better survive vermouth.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
