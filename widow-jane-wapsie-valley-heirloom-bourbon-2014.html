The short version

The rye-containing Wapsie Valley recipe is an important early Brooklyn grain experiment. Its documented production history is stronger than the available independent tasting record.

Exact identity: January 2014 Wapsie Valley single-expression heirloom bourbon. Later one-year, 45.5% ABV / 375 mL examples are separately documented; exact launch proof, format and handwritten age remain unconfirmed.

Background and history

The 2014 founder announcement positions Wapsie Valley beside two Bloody Butcher recipes, making the grain comparison central to this early own-distillation project.

Later Baby Jane descends from the Wapsie Valley/Bloody Butcher corn work, but the 2024 blended product is neither this recipe nor evidence of its exact old-bottle quality.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The founder announcement documents 60% Wapsie Valley corn, 25% rye and 15% heirloom barley. A separate database records a one-year, 45.5% ABV, 375 mL Wapsie Valley example. That is useful context but does not establish the January 2014 launch bottle’s exact format or handwritten age; those fields remain unconfirmed.
