The short version

A bold original Legacy blend with broad support for rye spice, creamy texture and complexity. Later-batch dryness and aromatic roughness qualify the praise.

Exact identity: Wiser’s / J.P. Wiser’s Legacy, original circa-2010 discontinued NAS / 45% ABV / 90 proof formulation, 750 mL; excludes 2025 Legacy Edition revival.

Background and history

Original-era records document Legacy around 2010; a syndicated announcement mentions a 2009 award, so 2010 is an era reference rather than an exact first distillation or first-sale date. It later disappeared from the recurring range.

The producer’s October 2, 2025 Legacy Edition revival is a separate identity and is excluded here. Later old-stock review comments also describe variation within the original range; an original-era photograph is used.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The original issuer text names rye and malt components, copper-pot distillation and new white-oak casks. Later technical recollections also describe corn and mixed column/pot streams, while several reviews simplify the recipe to rye/malt only. These accounts do not justify a precise universal 33% rye or whole-blend all-pot recipe. Toasted rather than charred oak is attributed specialist reporting, not proof that every later batch used an identical regime.

Critical review synthesis

Selfbuilt · Selfbuilt’s Whisky Analysis

Selfbuilt finds a bold, creamy, characterful palate with persistent sweet/spicy detail. Faint solvent-like aromas and a dry, somewhat bitter initial finish limit elegance.

Scope: February 13, 2017 review of original range, bottle purchased June 2015; sensory solvent wording is not a chemical finding.

cricklewood · Whisky Connosr

The later original-range bottle offers an appealing silky entry and assertive spice, but also drying astringency and only medium finish length.

Scope: March 18, 2019 review; reviewer identifies post-2015 label in comments. Does not establish unchanged 2010 recipe.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Rest a small neat pour to assess fruit, spice, texture and the drying finish in the published accounts.

Water / rocks

Add drops gradually or one small cube, comparing aroma and texture as it dilutes.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and bitters. A recurring Canadian blend is the cost comparison before mixing a limited bottle.

Manhattan / highball

Try a small serve with restrained vermouth or soda; a softer whisky can lose definition under assertive mixers.
