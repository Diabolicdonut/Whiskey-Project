A historical premium Canadian blend with useful spice and texture evidence. Different sample eras show different finish and sweetness judgments, so the score is provisional.

Exact identity: Crown Royal Reserve / formerly Special Reserve, the historical NAS forty-percent identity; separate from Reserve Aged 12 Years introduced in 2024.

Background and history

The City of Waterloo museum documents Special Reserve’s 1992 public launch, including a preserved bottle produced in November 1991 for that launch. Its manufacturing date is not a separate earlier retail introduction.

Null’s October 2008 review documents the Reserve relabeling. The 2024 age-stated transition marks a different dossier boundary; the old expression’s historical reviews and November 2015 U.S. USD 40 / 750 mL price remain separately scoped.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Christopher Null · Drinkhacker

Null finds a gentle, spicy, repeatedly enjoyable pour with limited complexity and a short but pleasant ending.

Scope: October 23, 2008; exact relabeled NAS Reserve at 80 proof.

Kara Newman · Wine Enthusiast

Newman finds sufficient layered sweet/nutty character for sipping, with a longer ending that turns toward roast and spice. That differs from the earlier short-finish record.

Scope: November 1, 2015 issue; exact NAS Reserve, 40%, 750 mL, USD 40 reference.

talexander · Whisky Connosr

This community reviewer likes the richer texture and spice but finds the later sweetness cloying, an important counterweight to the more favorable professional samples.

Scope: February 12, 2012; exact NAS Reserve at 40%; one detailed community sample, not aggregate consensus.
