The short version

A very inexpensive Canadian mixing whisky with uneven critical reception. Supporters find clean sweetness and spice; others describe light flavors and a brief finish. Price does not turn it into an elite sipper.

Exact identity: Current Black Velvet Original Canadian blend, 40% ABV / 80 proof; brand lineage begins in 1951.

Background and history

The producer dates Black Velvet to 1951, with Black Label renamed after distiller Jack Napier described its texture. This origin story is attributed to the brand rather than treated as an independently settled industrial history.

Heaven Hill acquired the distillery in 2019. The current core bottle remains distinct from older Reserve and flavored extensions; no unchanged 1951 recipe is assumed.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer describes partly matured high wines combined with base spirit before further aging in bourbon barrels. This blending-before-final-maturation method differs from blending all components only at bottling. A published approximate three-year core age is retained as context, not converted into a verified age statement for the photographed NAS bottle.

Critical review synthesis

Mark Gillespie · WhiskyCast

Gillespie describes a clean sweet aroma, buttery entry and cinnamon that recedes into licorice and honey. He finds good balance and a sustained ending, awarding 89 points after encountering it blind.

Scope: Undated 40% ABV core blend record; original page HTML inspected when text retrieval was blocked.

Johannes Lindblom · WhiskyRant

Lindblom finds a spirit-led aroma, faint fruit and caramel, and a quick dry ending. He prefers it to his eight-year Reserve sample but regards both as modest blends.

Scope: 2015-era core 40% ABV sample; vintage not equated to every current bottle.

Josh · Sipology

Josh finds sweet fruit and a light ending, some rough edges, and useful performance in an Old Fashioned or with ginger ale. His enthusiasm is stronger for the eight-year Reserve.

Scope: August 22, 2014 head-to-head; historical core sample.
