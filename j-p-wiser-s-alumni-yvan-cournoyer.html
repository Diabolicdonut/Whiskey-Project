Background and history

Corby’s September 24, 2019 Captains announcement identifies the twelve-year three-grain Cournoyer release. The recipe is described as inspired by the 1972 Carleton Tower blend; it is not whisky distilled in 1972.

The age honors the player’s number. Current producer stock imagery authenticates this expression, while the label rather than a generic shop field controls its strength.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The verified named label and matched 2020 reviews identify 40%, correcting the shop’s unsupported 42% specification. Contemporary technical reporting lists double-distilled corn, column-distilled malt, and both column and column/pot rye, with Canadian-whisky, bourbon and virgin-oak casks. The label simplifies this to bourbon/virgin barrels. Exact proportions, chronology and finish durations remain undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Selfbuilt · Selfbuilt’s Whisky Analysis

Selfbuilt describes refined dried-fruit and floral character over sweet corn, but a light mouthfeel and quick, mildly bitter ending. A request for higher strength qualifies the praise.

Scope: July 30, 2020 exact twelve-year / 40%.

André, Patrick and Martin · Québec Whisky

The panel finds appealing fruit and forceful rye spice. André and Martin specifically criticize diluted or weak texture; Patrick finds useful intensity and a medium spicy finish.

Scope: 2019–2020 twelve-year / 40%; differences within the panel retained.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Rest a small neat pour to assess fruit, spice, texture and the drying finish in the published accounts.

Water / rocks

Add drops gradually or one small cube, comparing aroma and texture as it dilutes.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and bitters. A recurring Canadian blend is the cost comparison before mixing a limited bottle.

Manhattan / highball

Try a small serve with restrained vermouth or soda; a softer whisky can lose definition under assertive mixers.
