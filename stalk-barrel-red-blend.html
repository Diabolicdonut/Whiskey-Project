The short version

The most convincing conventional Stalk & Barrel blend here: malty sweetness and rye spice work together with citrus, vanilla and dried fruit, though some tasters still find youthful oak and a short or rough edge.

Exact identity: Stalk & Barrel Red Blend at 43% ABV / 86 proof, the higher-tier Blue/Red blend built around a high proportion of Still Waters single malt and rye plus corn whisky.

Background and history

Red launched alongside Blue as the more complex, higher-strength member of the pair, replacing the earlier 1+11 era with a blend that more prominently showcases Still Waters' own malt and rye.

Later shelf presence does not make Red interchangeable with Three Barrel; Three Barrel is a separate 2020-era recipe and dossier.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Mark Gillespie · WhiskyCast

Finds a strong balance of malt, rye spice, honey, vanilla, dried fruit and citrus with a long spicy finish; scores it very highly.

Scope: July 2018; exact Red Blend at 43% ABV.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Finds wood and dusty rye spice, grain and malt sweetness in an interesting, delicious blend, while noting youthful astringency and room for more barrel time.

Scope: October 30, 2017; exact 43% sample.

Blair Phillips · Distiller

Praises the symmetry between sweet barley and floral rye, with rye spice, oak and citrus pith; Distiller's score is strongly positive.

Scope: Exact Red Blend editorial tasting at 43% ABV.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Best starting point; let the malt sweetness and rye spice open before adding water.

Rocks

One small cube should soften youthful oak without erasing the rye.

Highball

Works well as a robust Canadian highball, especially if kept only lightly diluted.

Old Fashioned

A natural fit: its rye spice and malt sweetness need little additional sugar.

Manhattan

Use a balanced sweet vermouth; the 43% strength has enough structure to stay present.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
