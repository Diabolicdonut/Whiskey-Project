Background and history

The contemporary Canadian Whisky history dates Sterling’s introduction to 1987 as a response to shortages of twelve-year stocks. Its early use of some much older whisky is historical context, not the age of today’s blend.

Sterling became a continuing family expression. The 2011 review mentions CAD 25.25 without an explicit offer size, so it is not used as numeric Cost. The original NAS designation is retained; the current producer claim is reported separately from a verified bottle age statement.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

De Kergommeaux finds generous texture and fruit with a useful medium-length ending, alongside small synthetic, spirit-like and astringent touches. He considers it useful for both sipping and mixing.

Scope: November 22, 2011, exact Sterling / 40%; historical recipe context, not a current-batch guarantee.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra finds useful light body and spice but uncomfortable heat, misplaced bitterness and a short ending. His strongest endorsement is for ginger-ale mixing.

Scope: August 14, 2011 account, rescored September 2016; exact Sterling / 40%.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Rocks / water

Old Fashioned / Manhattan

Start with restrained sweetener or vermouth and adjust to the whisky’s own sweetness.
