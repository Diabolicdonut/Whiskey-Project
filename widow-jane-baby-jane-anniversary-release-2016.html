The short version

A historically revealing one-year heirloom experiment whose unexpectedly rounded sweetness impressed two reviewers; its original half-bottle price makes drinking Value very weak.

Exact identity: 2016 one-year Baby Jane anniversary release; 75 bottles, 375 mL, 91 proof, as documented by Bourbon & Banter in March 2017. This is not the 2024 national blend.

Background and history

The fourth-anniversary release documents Baby Jane corn years before the 2024 national blend. Bourbon & Banter identifies a 75-bottle release at the October 14, 2016 anniversary event.

The age, bottle size and farm-distillate identity are the reasons for this separate record. Later blended Baby Jane reviews are not evidence for the one-year bottle.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The matched review documents pot-distilled Brooklyn bourbon aged one year, with a reported mash of 80% Baby Jane corn, 10% rye and 10% malted barley. It was offered at 91 proof in 375 mL bottles. This recipe belongs to the 2016 experimental identity and is not copied to the 2024 blend.

Critical review synthesis

Brent Joseph and Pops · Bourbon & Banter

Both reviewers find clear young corn/malt character alongside unexpectedly rounded maple-like sweetness. They disagree on buying: Brent sees interest for enthusiasts, while Pops regards the original price as far beyond an everyday drinking proposition.

Scope: 2016 one-year release; published March 9, 2017; both contributors at one outlet, not two independent publishers.

Two contributors at one outlet provide one publisher’s matched record. A surprising one-year sample is not proof that later, older Baby Jane is the same whiskey.
