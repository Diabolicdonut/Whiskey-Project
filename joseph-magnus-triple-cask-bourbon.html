The short version

Very good approaching excellent; good value at a substantial price.

The standard Joseph Magnus Bourbon combines a bourbon foundation with Oloroso, Pedro Ximénez and Cognac cask influence. The critical evidence supports substantial aromatic layers and a rich, recognizable bourbon palate. A drier or less expansive finish keeps the score at 7.8, near excellent rather than automatically elite. 1345

This is the 100-proof triple-cask bottle sold in NC at $99.95, not the higher-priced Cigar Blend, a Murray Hill Club expression or a particular single-barrel selection. Those names and their reviews must remain separate. 6

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The brand traces its name to Joseph Magnus’ nineteenth-century spirits business and was revived in 2015. The producer’s account centers on a preserved family bottle and the effort to develop a modern whiskey with a similarly layered character. That history explains the finishing direction without proving that the present bottle tastes identical to the old one. 2

The historical barrel photograph comes from the producer’s brand-history page. It illustrates the revived company’s maturation and blending context; it is not an assertion that every barrel shown is in the current blend or that the earlier site is its present production location.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The producer confirms 100 proof and finishing in the three named cask types. The current standard product page does not guarantee a ten- or twelve-year age statement. References to mature bourbon in the brand’s development story should not become a fixed age for every contemporary batch. 1

Reported Indiana sourcing is useful background, but exact grain proportions are not independently confirmed for all standard bottles. The index therefore uses Undisclosed. The quality question is the integration of base spirit and finish, including whether wine, sweetness and oak remain balanced.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Dark fruit, orange, chocolate or cake-like sweetness, with wine and mature oak layers.

Palate

Rich fruit and spice over recognizable bourbon, though one account finds less depth than the expansive nose promises.

Finish

Wine-influenced fruit, leather and dry oak. Tannin can shorten enjoyment even when the overall profile is layered.

Jordan Moskal · Breaking Bourbon · July 16, 2020

Jordan Moskal’s review finds appealing orange and plum-like aroma, sweet fruit and leather, then a drier ending. The finishes generally cooperate, but the palate and finish do not fulfill every promise of the nose. 3

Alex Wang · The Whiskey Shelf

Alex Wang finds compelling fruit and rancio-like complexity, yet less expansive development than in the separate Cigar Blend line. This is a strong standard finished bourbon, not evidence for importing the premium bottling’s ceiling. 4

Mike & Mike · Bourbon Culture · 2023

The 8/10 assessment emphasizes wine-led fruit and rich spice while retaining the underlying bourbon. It supplies independent support for the excellent-tier vicinity rather than treating finishing itself as an upgrade. 5

Where the judgments differ

The reviews largely agree about aromatic richness and successful cask integration. They differ in how much palate expansion and sustained sweetness remain after the oak arrives. Those limitations are the difference between a near-excellent 7.8 and a confidently elite 9.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Serving guidance is editorial inference from the independent flavor evidence. It is not a claimed consensus of professional bartenders.

Neat / water

Give a neat pour time to open before adding drops. The layered nose is a major part of the purchase proposition.

On ice

One large cube can ease tannin and intensity, but may compress the finishing detail. Decide after tasting neat.

Old Fashioned

Rich dark fruit can work with very restrained sweetness; a heavy syrup measure risks doubling down on the finish.

Manhattan

Use a small first test with a balanced vermouth. Competing wine influences may blur the bourbon’s own cask layers.

Highball / cola

Possible, but weak value for a serve that conceals the expensive finishing character.

Food / occasion

An after-dinner pour or a comparison with Double Oaked can show how different secondary casks change flavor.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

Bourbon Quality
7.8 / 10

Retain 7.8. Breaking Bourbon and the Whiskey Shelf find rich finished fruit with a less persuasive palate or finish than the aroma; Bourbon Culture adds strong support for layered flavor that preserves the base bourbon. It substantially exceeds the ordinary midpoint and avoids the low anchor’s failed integration. Its complexity outpaces standard Double Oaked, but does not match Michter’s ceiling across the whole sip. This is not Cigar Blend.

Value · NC ABC
6.5 / 10

Retain 6.5 at $99.95. Distinctive sherry-and-Cognac character earns good value for its style, with a meaningful premium over very good everyday bourbon.

Quality scale: 1 catastrophic · 2 extremely poor · 3 poor · 4 below average · 5 average · 6 good · 7 very good · 8 excellent · 9 elite · 10 reference-class. Quality scores are category-relative. A 9.0 Bourbon means elite within Bourbon; a 9.0 Rye means elite within Rye. The numbers are not claims that unlike whiskey styles taste equivalent.
