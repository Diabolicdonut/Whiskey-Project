The short version

A sweet, spiced Canadian blend with more character than its delicate body suggests. The published record likes its mixing ability but disagrees about finish length and how well it tolerates dilution.

Exact identity: Royal Canadian Small Batch Canadian Whisky, 40% ABV / 80 proof, NAS; the taller small-batch release introduced to the U.S. around 2010, distinct from the earlier inexpensive Royal Canadian blend.

Background and history

The original review describes a newly formulated, more expensive small-batch bottling and U.S. distribution before a planned Canadian introduction in 2011. The archive’s 2011 publication timestamp is retained separately from its earlier launch narrative.

Sazerac’s 2020 release associates its Canadian portfolio with Old Montreal. That portfolio statement does not establish where each component of the earlier Small Batch was distilled. A later reviewer’s suggestion that the brand began with the 2018 acquisition conflicts with the much earlier published record and is rejected.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

Finds extensive fruit and spice, developing weight and a moderately persistent finish, but also cloying sweetness, pepper heat, bitterness and thin patches. A flavorful mixer rather than an especially refined neat pour.

Scope: Original small-batch era; archive dated November 28, 2011, with an earlier launch narrative.

Victor · Whisky Connosr

Describes substantial sweetness, grain character and a fairly lasting finish. Praises the interplay of flavors while retaining excess alcohol bite, bitter wood and an untidy finish as limitations.

Scope: January 14, 2013; one bottle, not three independent reviews despite three scoring formats.

Kurt Bradley · The Gentleman’s Flavor

Finds a gentle, sweet baking-spice profile and a pleasant but brief finish; substantial dilution makes it too thin. This is a useful counterweight to older accounts of greater finish length.

Scope: Page updated May 16, 2023; sensory account only, not its inconsistent brand-origin claim.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat to assess the light body before adding water.

Rocks

Use a small cube; the later review specifically finds heavy dilution weakens it.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained sugar so the natural sweetness does not dominate.

Manhattan

Start at 2:1 whisky to sweet vermouth; reduce vermouth if the blend disappears.

Highball

Ginger ale suits the documented sweet-spice profile; keep the mixer short.
