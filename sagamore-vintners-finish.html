The short version

A 98.4-proof limited 2018 rye with dark cherry, spiced red wine, cocoa and dry wood notes supported by matched independent tasting. No trustworthy 2018 bottle-size-specific USD price has been verified, so Value and Overall remain N/A.

Exact identity: Sagamore Reserve Vintner's Finish Rye, spring 2018 Maryland/DC-only edition, 98.4 proof and 7 months in different pinot noir, shiraz, and port casks after four years in new oak. Different from 2019/2023 Port Finish rye.

Background and history

Released as a limited 2018 reserve project shortly after Sagamore's Baltimore distillery opened, when sourced rye still underpinned much of the brand.

One contemporaneous release article rounded the proof to 98, but the producer and matched Distiller identity both list 98.4; preserve exact 98.4 specification.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The early Sagamore finishing experiment used a four-year straight rye blend, separately rested in pinot noir, shiraz and port casks for seven months, and reblended before bottling at a documented 98.4 proof. The 2018 public release targeted Maryland and Washington DC.

Critical review synthesis

Jake Emen · Distiller

Independent expert record notes tart cherry and green apple on the nose, then wine, mulled spices, cocoa-rich cherries, butterscotch and dry oak on the palate. Describes a peppery medium-length finish with woody/tannic cedar aspects.

Scope: Exact 2018 Vintner's Finish, 98.4 proof, limited Maryland/DC edition.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Neat is most informative for the interplay between sour cherry wine aromatics, red fruit and rye spice.

Rocks

A large cube may reduce tannic oak but soften the complex fruit and cocoa contrasts.

Old Fashioned

Keep sweetener minimal to avoid overloading the dark fruit. Use aromatic bitters for structure.

Manhattan

Use relatively dry vermouth with restrained bitters; sweet vermouth can compound red-wine fruit.

Highball

Possible for mixing, but the limited bottle's special finishing deserves an initial neat comparison before dilution.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
