How to approach this expression

Serving recommendations are editorial inferences from this expression’s documented sensory profile and strength; they are not a claim of first-hand cocktail testing.

Neat: follow the development

Kentucky Vintage Bourbon is 90 proof. Use a small tulip glass and compare the opening with the middle: caramel with youthful alcohol. The key question is whether that character persists as buttery grain and smoky oak.

Water: adjust in small steps

At 90 proof, taste neat before adding water; dilution is an experiment, not an improvement guaranteed by the label. The relevant finishing behavior is wood and heat compete with sweetness. Stop early if the already gentle body becomes less distinct.

Old Fashioned: match the sweetness

The documented middle is buttery grain and smoky oak. Begin with less sugar than usual and taste before adding more. At 90 proof, restrained dilution preserves the body; the cocktail should still taste of Kentucky Vintage Bourbon.

Rocks or a restrained Sour

A separate pour over one large cube lets you track the change from caramel with youthful alcohol toward wood and heat compete with sweetness. For a Sour, balance citrus and sugar in a small trial; the documented finish is the part most easily obscured.
