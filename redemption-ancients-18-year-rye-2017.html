Redemption Ancients 18 Year Rye — 2017

An elite historical rye whose mature balance is widely admired, but wood-heavy aroma and variable mouthfeel prevent a forced perfect score.

Research current as of October 5, 2026

The short version

Exceptional rye; only slightly above fair value at $400 MSRP.

Redemption’s historical Ancients 18 Year Rye combines remarkable age with concentrated rye character that survives the oak. Bourbon Culture rates it 10/10 and The Whiskey Wash 9/10, while Drinkhacker’s A- account is more cautious about its woody nose. The combined evidence supports 9.1: exceptional, without forcing the seed target of 10. 123

The exact subject is the 109.9-proof rye released in 2017 from 1998-era Indiana stocks. A later Ancients 18 Year bourbon is a different whiskey. Shared age and range names cannot establish shared recipe, proof or quality.

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

The release drew on unusually old Seagram/MGP rye stocks. By bottling age, that distillate carried a historical origin beyond the more familiar young 95/5 rye expressions. This makes it an instructive comparison with standard Redemption Rye, but age and historical interest do not guarantee a better finish. 1

Bottle-count evidence is inconsistent: the retrospective review notes different figures on a placard and producer material. No definitive total is adopted. That uncertainty concerns release documentation, not the well-identified eighteen-year, 109.9-proof rye assessed by the independent critics.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The release is eighteen years old at 54.95% ABV, with the historical 95% rye / 5% malted-barley recipe reported. It is a barrel-proof straight rye, unlike standard 92-proof Redemption Rye or the later Ancients bourbon. The three critical sources identify the same historical expression. 132

The key maturation achievement is retaining herbs, spice and sweetness under very old oak. The best assessments find that balance unusually complete. The less enthusiastic aroma and lighter-mouthfeel observations are retained because they explain why exceptional quality is a stronger conclusion than universal perfection.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Mature cedar/wood with mint, dill or clove; some tasters find the oak unusually dominant before sweetness emerges.

Palate

Concentrated rye herbs, dark sweetness, citrus and baking spice under firm but generally controlled oak.

Finish

Long minty/spicy and seasoned-wood development, with sweet detail in the most favorable account. Mouthfeel is not uniformly lush.

Mike & Mike · Bourbon Culture · March 1, 2023

The 10/10 retrospective finds extraordinary mature balance and a very long evolving finish. Its strong claim is supported by a detailed exact-expression tasting, but is not adopted as the dossier score by itself. 1

Christopher Null · Drinkhacker · December 20, 2017

Christopher Null gives A-. He finds the nose markedly wood-heavy before the palate opens into deep spice and sweetness. That stage-specific limitation matters for an overall reference-class claim. 3

Margarett Waterbury · The Whiskey Wash · March 1, 2018

Margarett Waterbury gives 9/10, finding concentrated herbs/spice, firm controlled oak and a memorable ending. She also describes a thinner mouthfeel and finds water unhelpful, a meaningful independent qualification. 2

Where the judgments differ

The reviews agree about memorable mature rye character and a developed finish. They differ on how inviting the nose is and how much texture accompanies the concentration. Elite quality is independently supported; near-flawless balance across every stage is not. The honest calibration is 9.1 rather than an automatic 10.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: Dilution guidance incorporates The Whiskey Wash’s direct water assessment. Other serves and occasions are editorial inferences from the documented flavor profile.

Neat / water

Try a small neat pour first. The Whiskey Wash finds that water does little for the already lighter mouthfeel.

On ice

Chilling may suppress herbs and mature detail. Assess the neat baseline before adding a cube.

Old Fashioned

The structure can plausibly support it, but syrup and bitters may hide the old-stock development that carries the price.

Manhattan

A restrained premium Manhattan could work by inference; no exact-release professional test was established.

Highball / cola

A weak drinking-value use at the historical $400 benchmark, as the mixer conceals the elite nuance.

Food / occasion

Best as a mature-rye reference tasting, with the 2017 rye identity written down to distinguish the later bourbon.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

Rye Quality · provisional
10.0 / 10

The specific 2017, 109.9-proof release is the practical American rye best anchor. Bourbon Culture describes reference-level mature balance and an evolving finish; the Whiskey Wash supports concentrated, varied rye character with controlled oak; Drinkhacker finds rich palate development despite a wood-heavy nose. Thin texture in one account and oak grip remain disclosed. Its integration supplies the selected ceiling above Booker’s heat and Owl’s weaker finish, far beyond ordinary midpoint rye.

Value · Original MSRP
5.5 / 10

Retain 5.5 at the documented original $400 MSRP. Naming the practical quality ceiling does not make a costly limited release efficient drinking value; fair approaching good still reflects the premium for its last increments of mature character.

Quality is category-relative and excludes price or personal-palate weighting. Value uses the identified $400.00 750 mL reference. The scores are editorial synthesis, not averages of incompatible published rating systems.

Quality scale: 1 catastrophic · 2 extremely poor · 3 poor · 4 below average · 5 average · 6 good · 7 very good · 8 excellent · 9 elite · 10 reference-class. Quality scores are category-relative. A 9.0 Bourbon means elite within Bourbon; a 9.0 Rye means elite within Rye. The numbers are not claims that unlike whiskey styles taste equivalent.
