The short version

Sweet corn whisky shaped by three oak regimes. Enthusiastic reviews describe good integration and silky texture; a strong counter-review finds tannins and an aroma flaw. Taste before committing to a large bottle.

Exact identity: Bearface Triple Oak Canadian Whisky, seven-year expression at 42.5% ABV; current core release.

Background and history

Bearface launched in Canada in 2018. Breaking Bourbon dates the ongoing U.S. release to May 2022, so those dates describe different market milestones.

The brand’s shipping-container aging is a modern method of exposing selected casks to changing temperatures. It does not establish a separate whisky age or a disclosed distilling source.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

American ex-bourbon wood, French red-wine oak and toasted virgin Hungarian oak create the documented Triple Oak treatment. The producer describes seven years in the first wood before later casks, while Breaking Bourbon describes seven total years divided between stages. Both support the seven-year bottle identity; the exact stage duration remains unresolved.

Critical review synthesis

Nick Beiter · Breaking Bourbon

Beiter finds integrated dessert flavors, silky texture and a gentle spice ending. He regards the original USD 35 budget as attractive for the sweet, layered style.

Scope: September 8, 2022 seven-year / 85-proof supplied sample.

Kirk Miller · InsideHook

Miller finds corn sweetness joined by fruit and vanilla, with spice at the end and more complexity than he expects from an inexpensive Canadian bottle.

Scope: 2022 core Triple Oak review and blender interview.

Jacob Wirt · The Whiskey Wash

Wirt reports a marker-like aroma he checks in several glasses, then dominant wood tannins and bitterness. He sees potential but wants substantially better balance.

Scope: June 2025 published seven-year / 85-proof record; sample and batch equivalence not established.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Check the aroma first, then allow air and evaluate sweetness against tannin.

Rocks / water

Try one large cube; monitor whether cooling improves balance without stripping the corn character.

Old Fashioned

Begin with very little syrup because sweetness dominates the positive reviews.

Highball

Soda or restrained ginger ale can extend the oak-spice profile; avoid assuming it needs more sweetness.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
