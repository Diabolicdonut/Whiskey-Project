Larceny Small Batch Bourbon

A comfortable wheated bourbon with more character than its midpoint seed placement implies, but modest weight and finish.

The short version

Good bourbon and a very good everyday wheated value.

Larceny Small Batch is a good, accessible wheated bourbon rather than a neutral category-midpoint example. Its sweetness has nutty and floral detail, although texture and finish do not reach the depth of premium wheated bourbon. The 6.2 Quality score preserves that distinction: appealing standard bourbon, with limitations that remain visible. 13

At the regular NC $29.95 reference, it gives the buyer a different grain profile without a major premium. That price affects Value, not Quality. The subject is the standard 92-proof Small Batch bottle, separate from variable-proof Barrel Proof releases. 4

Background

The name, the era and the limits of the story

Heaven Hill introduced Larceny in 2012 as a wheated bourbon with a name and key motif associated with the John E. Fitzgerald story. The brand narrative is part of its identity, while the current drinking proposition is simpler: wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain in a widely distributed Kentucky bourbon. 5

The long-running standard bottle and the later barrel-proof series answer different questions. Higher proof, a batch code or a celebrated limited release should not be imported into this review. A current standard-expression assessment carries more weight here than the original launch-era review.

What is actually in the bottle?

Specifications without invented precision

The current producer specification is 92 proof. The commonly reported grain formula is 68% corn, 20% wheat and 12% malted barley. Exact percentages remain labeled as reported rather than converted into a producer guarantee. The current bottle does not promise the often-repeated six-year age, so the index records NAS. 51

Wheat can shift emphasis toward rounded grain sweetness, but it does not eliminate oak, spice or bitterness. The practical distinction in the reviews is a gentler framework with appealing nuttiness and a relatively light body, rather than an intrinsically superior recipe.

Critical synthesis

The flavor evidence, with disagreement intact

The flavor profile synthesizes the credited written reviews. It is not an unperformed first-person tasting. Historical and single-barrel scope stays explicit.

Nose

Caramel, honeyed grain, nutty or floral accents and moderate oak. An older review finds substantially more sawdust-like wood.

Palate

Sweet and generally balanced, with nuts and soft baking spice; body can feel lighter than the aroma promises.

Finish

Pleasant but limited development. Wood and dry spice may linger more than layered fruit.

Nick Beiter · Breaking Bourbon · August 23, 2024

Nick Beiter’s 2024 review gives 2.5/5. He emphasizes accessible sweetness and balance while identifying light texture and limited complexity. This is useful support for a modestly above-average standard bourbon. 1

Alex Wang · The Whiskey Shelf

Alex Wang finds more aromatic appeal in hay, floral sweetness and nutty confectionery notes. He still wants more weight and punch. That positive counterpoint raises the assessment above an automatic 5 without making it excellent. 3

Christopher Null · Drinkhacker · September 1, 2012

Christopher Null rates the 2012 launch B, finding noticeable woody imbalance. The dated review helps define the risk of dry oak, but does not establish that every current batch repeats the same severity. 6

Where the judgments differ

The difference is largely how much detail the drinker finds in the gentle profile, and whether oak reads as supportive structure or dryness. Recent balance and the stronger aromatic account support good quality; neither removes the repeated body/finish limitation. The seed target of 5 is therefore not adopted.

Practical use

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: These serves are editorial inferences from proof, sweetness and light texture. No expression-specific professional bartender consensus was established in the cited research.

Neat / water

Start neat. A few drops are enough to test openness; the already light body does not call for substantial dilution.

On ice

One large cube can make an easy relaxed pour. Let the first sips guide whether further dilution helps.

Old Fashioned

Nutty caramel works with modest sugar and aromatic bitters. Preserve whiskey volume rather than making a sweet dessert drink.

Manhattan

Use a restrained vermouth measure if the bourbon seems delicate. A heavy, bitter vermouth may overpower its softer framework.

Highball / cola

A short, chilled highball or bourbon-and-cola is plausible, though the extra mixer reduces the subtle floral distinction.

Food / occasion

A useful casual wheated pour beside toasted nuts or a lightly sweet dessert; pairings are editorial suggestions, not sensory tests.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

/ 10 · Native category anchors

Breaking Bourbon finds an approachable, pleasant but uncomplicated bourbon; the Whiskey Shelf’s fragrant, nutty aroma is stronger than its palate, while the older Drinkhacker launch review warns about wood imbalance. That places it modestly above the ordinary Maker’s/Bulleit midpoint, safely above Cleveland, and far below Michter’s mature ceiling. Its extra aromatic detail is real, but the limited body does not justify a clearly good tier.
