Elijah Craig Small Batch

Elijah Craig’s regular 94-proof bourbon remains a persuasive middle-shelf reference. Its current strengths are balance and familiar barrel flavor rather than a guaranteed age or dramatic intensity.

The short version

Classic oak and caramel, without the old twelve-year promise

Elijah Craig Small Batch occupies a useful place between basic sweet bourbon and a more mature, oak-driven style. Reviewers commonly find caramel, vanilla, nuts, citrus, toasted wood and cinnamon. The 94-proof strength offers a little more structure than a standard 90-proof bottle without the demands of the producer’s barrel-proof releases. 1 2

Its limitations are familiar ones for an everyday bourbon: moderate flavor density, restrained development and an ending that can be agreeable rather than memorable. The Whiskey Shelf finds more interest than Breaking Bourbon’s relatively blunt oak-and-vanilla reading. At $34.95 in NC, the resulting proposition is still strong, provided the bottle is assessed as current NAS Small Batch rather than as the discontinued 12 Year. 1 2

Background

The twelve-year statement was removed in 2016

Heaven Hill moved the regular Elijah Craig bourbon from a twelve-year age statement to a NAS Small Batch expression in 2016. Contemporary coverage describes the change and compares the newer blend with its predecessor. That historical eight-to-twelve-year range is context for the transition, not a perpetual guarantee for every bottle sold a decade later. 3

The brand’s account links its name to the eighteenth-century distiller Elijah Craig and the history of charred-barrel bourbon. Claims about the single person who first charred a barrel are better understood as brand heritage than settled invention history. The practical modern distinction is simpler: Small Batch, Barrel Proof and Toasted Barrel are different products. 4

What is actually in the bottle?

A moderate proof and producer-confirmed No. 3 char

The widely reported Heaven Hill bourbon recipe is 78% corn, 10% rye and 12% malted barley. Independent reviewers corroborate it, but the current product page does not publish the full numerical split. It is treated as reported information rather than a producer-confirmed recipe. Sharing those proportions with another Heaven Hill bourbon does not establish identical barrel selection. 2 4

The producer confirms 94 proof, or 47% ABV, and new oak with a No. 3 char. The current regular bottle has no numerical age statement and no disclosed secondary finish. Barrel selection and blending seek the familiar oak, vanilla and spice profile rather than advertising one fixed age. 4

This is a batched fixed-proof bourbon, not cask strength and not Bottled-in-Bond. A recurring barrel-count claim in older reporting should not be treated as a current legal definition of small batch. The current specification does not establish one exact blend age, warehouse location or filtration regime for all bottles.

Critical synthesis

A flavor profile, with the disagreements intact

These notes synthesize the named reviews below; they are not a first-person tasting of a newly opened bottle. Older reviews supply perspective on the standard expression, while current specifications come from current product sources.

Nose

Vanilla, caramel, nuts and toasted oak, with citrus, coffee or chocolate detail in some accounts.

Palate

Sweetness and oak remain central. Body can feel comfortably rounded even when the flavor sequence is relatively straightforward.

Finish

Cinnamon, dry oak and mild heat linger. The close is usually pleasant, though its depth and complexity are modest.

Breaking Bourbon · 2019

The review finds vanilla, caramel, nuts and oak, with a rounded mouthfeel but relatively simple flavor development. Cinnamon and wood linger in a satisfying yet ordinary finish. It is solid bourbon without the predecessor’s old-age distinction. 1

The Whiskey Shelf

This account finds more detail in caramel, coffee, chocolate, citrus, nuts and warm spices. It praises a decently rich, enjoyable bourbon while stopping well short of the concentration available in Elijah Craig Barrel Proof. 2

The Whiskey Reviewer · John Rayls, 2016

The transition-era review describes honey, caramel and oaky vanilla with a lingering spicy close. It regards the NAS release as a worthwhile bourbon, even while acknowledging that the older twelve-year expression had advantages. 3

Where the judgments differ

The range is between a simple, agreeable bourbon and a richer everyday sipper with useful roasted detail. Neither view requires the current bottle to reproduce the old twelve-year whiskey. A drinker who enjoys traditional caramel and oak may find enough interest; someone seeking a long series of flavor changes may prefer a more concentrated or age-stated expression.

Best ways to enjoy

Put the bottle in the right glass

Evidence and inference: The producer explicitly features an Old Fashioned, and the reviews describe moderate body and familiar oak. Other build recommendations are editorial inferences from those traits. 4 1 2

Neat / a few drops of water

Begin neat and allow the nut, coffee or citrus accents to emerge around the basic caramel and vanilla. A few drops of water may ease spice without sacrificing much, but the bottle is already at a manageable proof. The moderate richness is better preserved by limited dilution than by a rapidly melting pile of ice.

One large cube

Cooling can mellow spice and oak, but heavy dilution may simplify an already direct palate. One cube preserves moderate richness while letting the finish soften gradually.

Old Fashioned

The producer specifically emphasizes this cocktail. Editorially, vanilla, caramel and oak need modest sugar rather than a dessert build; bitters can reinforce warm spice without hiding the nuts.

Whiskey Sour

Editorial inference: citrus complements the orange or lemon impressions in the reviews. Keep sweetness restrained so the traditional barrel character remains present after shaking.

An Old Fashioned suits the traditional oak-and-caramel profile, while a sour can complement the citrus notes described by reviewers. Its price makes mixing reasonable, though a cheaper bonded bourbon can cover heavily flavored drinks. Choose Small Batch when its particular balance will be tasted, and assess Barrel Proof separately when more concentration is the goal.

Where it is less useful

Less ideal: paying an age-stated premium for this NAS bottle, or using so much sweetener that its oak balance becomes irrelevant.

Final assessment

Two scores, two different questions

Bourbon Quality
6.2 / 10

Breaking Bourbon describes rounded, enjoyable sweetness and oak without a standout feature; the Whiskey Shelf finds balanced fruit and sufficient richness, with an older NAS-era review adding context. This clears the ordinary Maker’s/Bulleit midpoint through body and coherence, and is safely removed from the chemical low anchor. It stays near Four Roses Small Batch, below Knob Creek 9’s mature definition and well below Michter’s long, layered ceiling.

Value · NC ABC
7.8 / 10

At $34.95, the rounded 94-proof profile remains very good value, approaching excellent. Knob Creek 9 and Maker’s 46 at $39.95 create credible upgrades, so the buying conclusion is slightly more restrained than before.
