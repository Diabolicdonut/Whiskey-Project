The short version

An inexpensive, thick caramel flavor extension with useful mixing potential. Critics agree on sweetness; they disagree on whether the whisky and spice balance it sufficiently for neat drinking.

Exact identity: Black Velvet Toasted Caramel, natural-caramel-flavored Canadian whisky at 35% ABV / 70 proof; U.S. launch September 2012.

Background and history

The producer announcement places the U.S. debut in September 2012. It was the brand’s first flavor extension and preceded Cinnamon Rush, Apple and Peach.

Current official range and NC regular-price records retain the expression. Historical reviews remain sample-specific rather than proof of a perfectly unchanged recipe since launch.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The launch announcement confirms natural toasted-caramel flavoring added to Black Velvet whisky and 70 proof. The current photographed label is NAS, while NC lists its SKU as 3Y. Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The specialist finds substantial caramel sweetness increasingly balanced by pepper, citrus bitterness and creamy texture. He reports a persistent ending with recognizable whisky spice.

Scope: September 2012 exact 35% ABV expression.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra finds the flavor cloying and the underlying whisky too subdued for enjoyable neat drinking. Ice and ginger ale improve it; his 75.5-point assessment is chiefly as a mixer.

Scope: March 28, 2013 exact 35% ABV.

Kara Newman · Wine Enthusiast

Newman describes a viscous sweet caramel profile with oak and a little finishing spice, suggesting dessert or warming mixed drinks.

Scope: March 2013 review, September 2012 launch identity.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Taste a little before committing; the full reviews disagree on tolerable sweetness.

Rocks / water

Ice is directly supported by the less enthusiastic reviewer as a way to moderate cloying character.

Old Fashioned

Use a small flavored fraction with ordinary whisky and skip syrup initially.

Highball

Try ginger ale with plenty of ice, or less-sweet soda if the caramel is already dominant.
