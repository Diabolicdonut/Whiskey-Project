The short version

One of the better-supported mature Canadian blends in this expansion. Independent records praise richness, texture and long development, with a smaller caution about excess oak at the tail of the finish.

Exact identity: Canadian Club Chronicles Issue No. 3, The Speakeasy, 43-year label statement, 45% ABV / 90 proof / 750 mL; 2020 release.

Background and history

The producer announced The Speakeasy in October 2020 as the third Chronicles release. Its name evokes clandestine bars in the Prohibition-era brand narrative; that narrative is separated from current sensory evidence.

The Canadian launch SRP was CAD 319.99 / 750 mL. K&L’s exact U.S. archive says previously USD 300, but supplies no defensible price-period date. The latter is an archival context checked October 14, 2026, not a current regular quote.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

The detailed record finds a slowly opening aroma, rich creamy texture, restrained herbal spice and a long warm ending. Mature wood supports the development rather than erasing its delicacy.

Scope: November 18, 2020, 43-year / 45% ABV exact Speakeasy.

Chip Dykstra · The Rum Howler Blog

Dykstra describes unusually rich aromatics, luxurious texture and sustained grain, spice and fruit. He regards the restrained rye contribution as an improvement over earlier addition recipes.

Scope: January 4, 2021, exact 43-year / 45% ABV; 95 points include presentation.

David S · Bourbon By Proxy

The reviewer finds creamy sweetness and mature leather/wood, with a long finish. His main reservation is that oak becomes slightly excessive at the very end.

Scope: 2022 dated exact 90-proof / 43-year review; tentative production and folklore claims not adopted.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Begin neat with a small pour and allow time in the glass to evaluate wood, grain and fruit integration.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops of water before a cube; protect the documented texture and subtle mature flavors.

Old Fashioned

A small Manhattan or restrained Old Fashioned is possible, but taste neat before deciding to sweeten an expensive release.

Highball

Use a small measure of soda if desired; the old-stock premium does not automatically make it a better mixing whisky.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
