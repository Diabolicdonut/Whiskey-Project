The short version

A documented bourbon/agave collaboration with two independently described neat-drinking cases. The cask influence adds a specific contrast while the base bourbon remains identifiable.

Exact identity: Widow Jane Tequila Ocho Cask Finish, launched May 2026; ten-year base bourbon, eight-month tequila-cask finish, 91 proof.

Background and history

Tequila Ocho first used Widow Jane bourbon barrels for tequila releases in 2022 and 2023. The 2026 bourbon reverses that relationship by using Ocho casks for secondary maturation.

The producer identifies this as Ocho’s first release of aged-tequila casks for whiskey production. That claim is attributed to the producer; the collaboration does not turn the bourbon into tequila or introduce a mezcal blending addition.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer documents its ten-year flagship bourbon base finished for eight months in casks previously used for aged Tequila Ocho. It is non-chill filtered and bottled at 45.5% ABV. Bourbon mash bills and individual component distilleries remain undisclosed.

Critical review synthesis

Joe Miragliotta · Joe’s Daily

Miragliotta describes caramel/oak and dark fruit, followed by citrus/stone-fruit brightness and restrained roasted agave. He finds the mouthfeel unexpectedly silky and the finish integrated rather than masking the bourbon.

Scope: May 2026 release, 91 proof.

Paul Orchard · The Northwest Beer Guide

Orchard independently reports tea/citrus/tobacco aromas, pecan and chocolate with mango/agave on the palate, then coffee, floral and pepper notes. He favors neat or modest ice over heavy cocktails.

Scope: July 29, 2026 exact Ocho-finish sample; supplied bottle disclosed.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Start neat and allow a little time, as the two reviews favor inspecting the bourbon/agave interaction directly.

Rocks / water

Try a few drops or a single large cube after the neat sample; stop before its aromatic contrast disappears.

Old Fashioned

Use minimal syrup and a small bitters measure; the cask influence is the feature worth preserving.

Manhattan

Test a light vermouth in a small two-to-one trial; a very rich modifier may bury the agave contrast.

Serving suggestions are editorial starting points, grounded in documented strength and style; adjust to your taste.
