The short version

A verified all-rye Canadian whisky with a modest price and sharply divided reviews. One critic finds lasting spice and creaminess; another finds solvent-like youth and a quick ending. It is a useful grain comparison, not an American straight-rye proxy.

Exact identity: Canadian Club 100% Rye / Canadian Chairman’s Select 100% Rye, 40% ABV, NAS; Canada 2014, U.S. producer launch May 24, 2016.

Background and history

The Canadian release began in 2014. The producer’s U.S. announcement is May 24, 2016; a secondary June rollout description is not treated as a conflicting Canadian first-release date.

Eric Burke directly compared purchased Canadian and U.S. bottles in 2016 and found the same tasting profile. This supports one dossier for the Chairman’s Select and U.S. naming, while not guaranteeing perfect later batch equivalence.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The producer names Alberta Distillers and confirms an all-rye grain bill. New white American oak, once-used bourbon barrels and Canadian-whisky barrels supply the components blended to taste. It is bottled at 80 proof with no age statement. This recipe is distinct from Canadian Club’s Ontario multi-grain blends.

Critical review synthesis

Blair Phillips · Distiller

Phillips describes floral grain, vanilla, creamy caramel and persistent citrus-spice character. He regards it as effective for cocktails and enjoyable neat, rating his sample at 88 points.

Scope: 40% ABV U.S. 100% Rye entry, linked to Canadian Chairman’s Select.

The Scotch Noob · The Scotch Noob

The critic finds a promising sweet aroma undermined by solvent notes and excess heat. Creamy body does not offset sparse development or a brief finish; water offers limited improvement.

Scope: December 26, 2016 exact 40% ABV / 750 mL purchased U.S. sample.

Eric Burke · Bourbon Guy

Burke finds the Canadian and American bottles equivalent to his palate, good rather than great, and below his preferred alternatives. This is a direct comparison, not a production guarantee.

Scope: August 9, 2016 cross-market purchased-bottle comparison.

Serving recommendations

Neat

Taste for the disputed raw aroma and heat before judging it as a neat sipper.

Rocks / water

Try water a few drops at a time; the negative review finds only a small improvement.

Old Fashioned

Use restrained syrup and aromatic bitters to test whether the rye spice carries the drink.

Highball

Ginger ale can suit a soft Canadian rye; use a modest mixer ratio so grain character remains.
