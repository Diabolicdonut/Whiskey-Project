Exact identity: Forty Creek Three Grain Harmony, 2015 ninth annual limited edition / Lot 2015, 43% ABV / 86 proof, NAS; 9,000-bottle corn/rye/barley blend.

Background and history

The September 25, 2015 contemporary review identifies the ninth annual special release, 9,000 bottles and CAD 69.95 at LCBO. The producer’s Lot 2015 image confirms forty-three percent.

This is distinct from the original Three Grain and the later 2020 twentieth-anniversary reprise. Mature components, a series number and the release year do not become the final whisky’s age.

For the shared historical context, read Canadian whisky, cross-border trade and the American market. The release chronology above distinguishes the current bottle from its brand’s earlier history.

Producer, grains, aging and finishing

The contemporary account attributes a thirty-barrel blend with some stocks dating to 1992 and four-year corn added for body. Older rye component ages differ across later secondary accounts, so no exact whole-blend age or twenty-two-year statement is assigned. Grain maturation precedes blending; no distinct wine-cask finish is authenticated by a wine-like tasting note.

Critical review synthesis

Davin de Kergommeaux · Canadian Whisky

De Kergommeaux finds substantial fruit and spice development, a creamy buffer against heat and a long, satisfying conclusion. The assessment is strongly favorable.

Scope: September 25, 2015; exact Harmony release and attributed Hall component interview.

Eric Burke · BOURBON GUY

Burke finds unusually coherent blending, soft texture and well-managed spice despite prominent dried fruit, with a medium-length ending.

Scope: February 18, 2016; exact bottle 3764 / 43%, obtained by bottle swap; price explicitly Canadian.

Bryan Vanderkruk · Toronto Whisky Society

Vanderkruk finds familiar sweet grain and nutty spice but a short ending and less impressive development than the strongest accounts. His acetone-like aroma impression is not an analytical finding.

Scope: May 21, 2017; separate 2015 Harmony / 43% section, not adjacent Port Wood or Founder’s Reserve reviews.

André, Patrick and Martin · Québec Whisky

Patrick welcomes rich spice and length, while André finds muted development and Martin reports uneven flavor success and limited persistence. This preserves the divided sample record.

Scope: Three complete exact 2015 / 43% accounts on one publisher page; no pooled population consensus claimed.
