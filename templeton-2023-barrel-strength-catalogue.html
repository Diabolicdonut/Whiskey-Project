Reviewer Brad praises the concentration and complexity but also describes assertive proof warmth and wood. The editor's 7.1 is a provisional rye-category judgment from those documented observations, not a numerical average of review grades or a personal tasting.

Best ways to enjoy this release

Serving guidance follows the named 114.8-proof bottle and independent tasting; these are editorial uses, not claims of personally tested cocktail outcomes.

Neat — first reference

Begin with a small, rested pour. Compare the caramel/vanilla opening with the review's pepper, oak and savory rye intensity. At 114.8 proof this is a deliberate sipping rye rather than a gentle beginner pour.

A few drops of water

Test dilution incrementally to manage the independently described heat and tannic oak. Whether it enhances the fruit or thins the finish is an experiment, not a sourced outcome.

On a large cube

Reasonable for a long sip if barrel-strength warmth dominates. Slow dilution can restrain pepper and oak; do not assume it becomes sweeter without trying it.

Spice-led Old Fashioned

Try a restrained sugar dose and aromatic bitters if the peppery backbone is desired. The independent reviewer enjoys the rye producer's style in Old Fashioneds, but this higher-proof limited edition was principally evaluated as a sipping pour.

Avoid: heavy sweetness or toasted additions that bury a $60 limited-edition rye's already substantial oak and spice.
